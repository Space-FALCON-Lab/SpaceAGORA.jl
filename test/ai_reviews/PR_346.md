# PR346 review: thruster gimbals

## Scope

Review of the SpaceAGORA `thruster-gimbal` branch against main (it was built
on `sensors`, PR #339, merged as ebda48836, and then merged with main). Step
7d of the actuator plan, first part: the gimballed engine; the control
moment gyro follows separately. The code with its tests, the docs, a merge
of main, then the review fix below.

Authorship disclosure: this record was drafted by the author of the PR. The

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings. Points a reviewer should check:

- Low, by design: a gimbal turns the direction about the nozzle location
  (the pivot); the nozzle does not move, and the gimbal's own mass and the
  engine's moving mass are not modelled.
- Low, by design: gimbal angles change at control ticks, between steps, as
  hinge angles do; there is no gimbal dynamics or actuator torque.
- Low: the scene export draws the plume along the thruster's nominal
  direction (the scene geometry is static).
- Fixed after the automated review of d9ddfb2c3 (7c8dfd063), P1: in
  `:mounted` actuation `ApolloDescentControlModel` returned one level per
  thruster (`thruster_force_n`, sized to its jet layout), while
  `mounted_wrench`, `apply_hinge_levels!` and `apply_gimbal_levels!` need
  one level per mounted actuator. A lander with a gimbal therefore stopped
  at its first control tick, and on main a lander with any other
  non-thruster actuator (a hinge, wheel, rod, laser or user-defined kind)
  already stopped in `mounted_wrench`. The controller's mounted level vector
  now covers the whole mounting order: the thrusters' levels first, as
  before, and every other mounted actuator holding (a hinge or gimbal at its
  current angle, refreshed at each control cycle, other kinds at zero), also
  when the spacecraft is inactive or has touched down, where only the
  thrusters are switched off. `control_thruster_levels`, which the viewer
  reads, stays one level per thruster. A lander with thrusters only keeps the
  vector it had, so its runs are bitwise unchanged (the Apollo fingerprint).
- Low, follow-up: the descent controller does not trim the engine with a
  gimbal (it holds the gimbal); its own allocation reads the jets' nominal
  `direction`.
- Low, as for hinges: a checkpoint file does not hold gimbal angles; a
  resumed run starts from the angles the spacecraft record holds.

## P1 Assessment

No P1 findings. Without a gimbal the thruster's direction is used as before
(a Boolean guard, not a multiplication by the identity), so the wrench,
allocation and propellant are bitwise unchanged; the comparison below shows
the four fingerprints identical against the base.
