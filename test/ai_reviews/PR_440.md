# Review record: PR 440: Take a periodic tick at a checkpoint boundary it misses only by rounding

## Scope

Review of the SpaceAGORA `tick-clock-decimal-boundaries` branch against main
(4f727266b): the fix for finding 3 (P2) of the outside review of 2026-10-20,
"decimal checkpoint boundaries can abort the next segment's tick clock", in the run
clock that #413 added for periodic navigation, guidance and control ticks.

The run clock picked the next tick after a segment's start, and the tick at a
segment's end, by exact floating-point comparison. A checkpoint boundary and the grid
time meant to fall on it can differ by an ulp: `3 * 0.1` is 0.30000000000000004, and a
0.3 s checkpoint interval ends the first segment at 0.3. The first segment then ended
without that tick, and the second scheduled it an ulp after its start. The solver
rejected that step and aborted as unstable (`dt was forced below floating point
epsilon`), so `run_simulation` threw `Checkpointed solve failed with
retcode=Unstable.` Reproduced on main with the reviewer's run (navigation every 0.1 s,
0.3 s checkpoints, 1.5 s mission: two navigation ticks, then the abort) and with a
boundary at `prevfloat(0.5)` and ticks every 0.25 s. The same abort comes from the
checkpoint loop alone: ten 0.1 s intervals reach 0.9999999999999999, so a 1 s run ends
with a segment one ulp long, which also aborted on main (no tick involved). On main,
with the tick 1 to 2 ulps after a segment's start the run aborted; from 3 ulps on it
completed.

The fix reconciles the two times on both sides of the checkpoint:

- `SimulationCallbacks._same_run_time(a, b)`: two run times within 100 units in the
  last place of the larger are one time. This is the tolerance OrdinaryDiffEqCore
  4.18.0's `modify_dt_for_tstops!` gives a step that reaches a stop. It covers the
  rounding of `k * Δt` and of a boundary, an ulp or two each. 100 ulps of a year in
  seconds is under a microsecond, far below any tick period.
- Under the run clock (a checkpointed or resumed run) only:
  - a tick within that tolerance of a solve's final time adds no stop of its own and
    fires at the final time, before the rows saved and the checkpoint written there;
  - at a solve's end (its final time, or where `terminate!` stopped it) the condition
    accepts a tick within the tolerance. Elsewhere in a solve the comparison stays
    exact, so ticks of different rates an ulp apart inside a segment keep their own
    times, as in a run in one piece;
  - a tick within the tolerance of the run's end belongs to the run's end: the run
    does not take it, and the end-of-run checkpoint's pending entry covers it;
  - at a solve's start a tick within the tolerance of the start is the one taken at
    the previous solve's end, or the pending one, which the resumed run takes there.
- `SimulationEngine._checkpoint_segment_end`: a segment that would end within the
  tolerance of the run's end ends at the run's end, so no last segment is an ulp or
  two long and no boundary checkpoint sits that close to the run's end.

The checkpoint records what each clock did. A run lists its periodic clocks in the
shared buffers, in the order of their callbacks. Every checkpoint it writes carries a
version 2 entry, `runtime_state.periodic_ticks = (version=2, time_s=t,
pending=[(clock=i, period_s=Δt), ...])`. The entry names the clocks whose next tick,
not yet taken, is due at its time (up to the tolerance on both sides):
- at the run's end, every clock with a tick there;
- at a segment boundary, none, since those ticks ran before the checkpoint;
- where a solve stopped early, those whose tick did not run before the stop.

A resumed run takes the ticks of the listed clocks at its start, once, matching them
to its own clocks by position and period, and takes no other clock's.

A checkpoint without the entry (0.4.0 and earlier) is read as 0.4.0 left it. 0.4.0
compared tick times exactly. It took a tick at or just before a boundary at that
tick's own time, and left a tick just after it (`3 * 0.1` after a boundary at 0.3) to
the next segment, which then aborted. That tick is now taken once, at the start of a
run resumed from such a checkpoint. 0.4.0's version 1 entry at the run's end left the
ticks exactly at that time pending, and still does; a tick just after it is pending
too. Before the entry (8db9b3172), a boundary checkpoint held none, so a later version
could not have told one written with the tolerance from one written by 0.4.0.

Without the run clock (a run solved in one piece, or a solve outside the engine)
nothing changes: the callback is `PeriodicCallback` with exact comparisons.

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings.

Points a reviewer should check:

- By design: in a checkpointed run a tick within the tolerance of a boundary now runs at the boundary's time instead of its grid time (0.3 instead of 0.30000000000000004). The two differ by at most 100 ulps; tick counts and order are those of a run in one piece, and states agree to rounding. With a boundary exactly on the grid, as with binary periods and intervals, nothing changes.
- By design: in a checkpointed run a tick within the tolerance before the run's end is no longer taken before the end; it is pending with the ticks at the end. A run solved in one piece still takes it (`PeriodicCallback`).
- By design: segment ends are still accumulated, `t + interval`. Over many decimal segments a boundary drifts from the decimal grid by about half an ulp per segment and passes the tolerance after several hundred segments. The tick then keeps its own stop, at least 100 ulps after the boundary. On main a step of 3 ulps or more after a segment's start did not abort.
- Fixed in 81752e40a, Giusy's decision of 2026-10-20 (it was Low at 8db9b3172): a checkpoint written by 0.4.0 at a boundary an ulp before a tick, which is the one left when 0.4.0's next segment aborted, held no record of that tick, and the branch counted it as taken. Every checkpoint now carries the version 2 entry, and one without it is read as 0.4.0 left it: the tick just after its time is taken at the resumed run's start, once (Scope).
- Fixed in d10ef9845, citing the fixed-head review of 2026-10-20 (P2 at 141617a34, "#440 can omit a control tick from a newly written early-stop checkpoint"): the entry decided pending or taken from the checkpoint's time alone, pending only at `t >= mission_end`. A solve stopped just before the run's end (at `prevfloat(1.0)` with a 1 s end) left the end's ticks, which the clock leaves to the run's end, yet the checkpoint called them taken. A solve stopped by a callback that runs after the periodic ones cut off a tick an ulp later (`3 * 0.1` after a stop at 0.3 s), and the checkpoint called it taken too. Either way the resumed run lost a control tick and its final wheel momentum differed from an uninterrupted continuation. The entry now names the clocks that had not taken the tick due then, read from each clock's state when the checkpoint is written, and the resumed run replays exactly those (Scope). Clocks that ran at the stop, such as the 0.3 s ticks in the second case, are not listed and do not run again.
- By design: since every checkpoint now carries `runtime_state.periodic_ticks`, a boundary or early-stop checkpoint has a `runtime_state` where it had none for a run without interlinks, batteries, faults, sensors, hinges, gimbals or model state. The engine writes the entry in its checkpoint loop; `_write_checkpoint!` called directly, as tests do for synthetic checkpoints, still writes none.
- Low: a checkpoint from 0.3.x, whose segments restarted the tick grid, is also read as 0.4.0 left it: a tick of the run's grid just after its time is taken at the resumed run's start.
- Correction to #413's record and test comment: `30 * 0.1` is exactly 3.0, so #413's "0.1 s period with 1 s segments" test had no tick near a boundary. The comment is fixed, and the test now counts the 3 s tick as well.
- Checked modes: the gravity-backbone mode refuses periodic GNC effectors, so only the segment-end rule applies there. On main and on the branch, its checkpointed runs with 0.1 s, 0.3 s and `prevfloat(0.5)` intervals complete and end within rounding of the run in one piece. The multirate mode aborted on main with 0.3 s checkpoints and 0.1 s navigation; on the branch it completes. Its periodic ticks remain wrong with or without checkpoints, as before: the sub-solves pass `reinit_callbacks=false` and revisit intervals, so control ticks repeat.
