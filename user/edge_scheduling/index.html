Edge Scheduling

This page covers planning which laser links of a constellation to activate over a horizon so that its formation is easy to reshape: the feasible-edge graph of each step, a linear model of the window, the finite-horizon controllability Gramian, and schedules that make it large.

What to read next:

  • Interlinks
  • Constellations

From a propagated constellation to a schedule

A run's interlink scheduler picks links step by step from what each link does now (see Interlinks). Edge scheduling plans over a window instead. The pieces are:

  1. Propagate the constellation, for instance with run_simulation(args; return_solution=true).
  2. At each planning step k, evaluate the feasible-edge graph G_k: which spacecraft pairs could carry a link (feasible_edge_graph).
  3. Build a linear time-varying model of the window, δx_{k+1} = A_k δx_k + Σ_e B_k^e δu_{k,e}, with one input block per candidate link, in any state representation (EdgeLinearModel).
  4. Project the window's end state on the coordinates that matter: the formation's shape, in one unit (see What the links cannot reach).
  5. Choose the active links σ_k ⊆ E_k, with |σ_k| ≤ n_max, to maximize a metric of the Gramian (schedule_edges).

The pieces meet only through plain data: the graphs, and the matrices of the linear model. A Cartesian, orbital-element or Kustaanheimo–Stiefel model plugs in the same way. In a model whose spacecraft keep their own time variables, such as Kustaanheimo–Stiefel fictitious time, each step must span one common physical-time interval for every spacecraft, so that both ends of a link are forced together.

The feasible-edge graph

An EdgeFeasibilityModel holds the rule. An edge (i, j) is feasible when:

  • Line of sight. With the link's occultation, the segment between the two spacecraft clears the planet's sphere, raised by min_ray_altitude_m, as for the link in a run.
  • Range against power. 0 < ρ_ij ≤ min(range_m, ρ_max(P_ij)). P_ij is the pair's transmitted power, and ρ_max is the max_range rule.
  • Field of regard at both ends. Some terminal of i sees the line of sight to j within its field of regard, and some terminal of j sees the line of sight back to i.
  • Terminal and duty-cycle limits. Links active together need distinct terminals at each end, and a DutyCycleLimit caps how often links may be active.
beam = GaussianBeam(wavelength_m=1.064e-6, waist_radius_m=0.2, aperture_radius_m=0.2)
link = LaserBeamLink(power_w=10_000.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3, beam=beam,
    occultation=(; min_ray_altitude_m=100e3))
fore = OpticalTerminal(boresight=[0.0, 1.0, 0.0], field_of_regard_rad=deg2rad(70.0))
aft = OpticalTerminal(boresight=[0.0, -1.0, 0.0], field_of_regard_rad=deg2rad(70.0))
feasibility = EdgeFeasibilityModel(link, [[fore, aft] for _ in spacecraft];
    max_range=MinimumForceRange(min_force_n=2e-3),
    duty_cycle=DutyCycleLimit(on_steps=4, window_steps=6))

solution = run_simulation(args; return_solution=true)
graphs = feasible_edge_graphs(feasibility, solution, times; planet=planet)
feasible_pairs(graphs[1])

A FeasibleEdgeGraph keeps each constraint's flag for every candidate pair (line_of_sight, in_range, field_of_regard, duty_cycle), the separation and its limit, the unit line of sight direction, and the terminals that can point along it. A single sample time takes feasible_edge_graph(feasibility, samples; context=LinkContext(time_s=t, planet=planet)), with one StateSample per spacecraft or a run state.

The link is a LaserBeamLink or a LaserMomentumLink. Its own occultation and range_m apply. The field of regard comes from the terminals, so the link carries no terminal pointing or mounted recoil. pairs defaults to every pair (all_pairs). The ring_pairs and grid_pairs lists of a constellation work too.

Range against power

max_range gives ρ_max(P):

  • MinimumForceRange(min_force_n=F): the largest separation at which the link still pushes with at least F newtons. With a GaussianBeam, the force falls with the beam's capture fraction at the far aperture, and ρ_max follows in closed form. Without a beam, the force does not fall with range.
  • Any function of the power in watts that returns metres.
  • A positive number of metres: a cap that does not depend on the power.
  • nothing, the default: only the link's range_m.

power_limited_range_m(rule, link, power_w) evaluates the rule. Each pair's power is the link's power_w unless power_w gives one per pair, as a vector or a function (i, j) -> P.

Terminals and the field of regard

Each spacecraft has a list of OpticalTerminals. A terminal steers within field_of_regard_rad of its boresight, given in one of three frames:

  • :rtn, the default: the spacecraft's radial, along-track and cross-track axes, for a spacecraft that holds a local-orbital attitude;
  • :body: through the sample's attitude, as the link's own terminal pointing reads mounted hardware;
  • :inertial.

A terminal carries one link at a time. terminal_assignment(graph, active) finds distinct terminals for a set of active links, or nothing when none exist. admissible_edge_set(graph, active; max_active, previous) checks a whole step: line of sight, range, field of regard, the count limit, the terminals and the duty cycle.

Duty cycle

DutyCycleLimit(on_steps=n, window_steps=w) allows at most n active link-steps in any w consecutive steps. With scope=:spacecraft (the default), the count is per spacecraft, and each of its active links counts once per step. With scope=:edge, the count is per link. A graph evaluated with previous (the active sets of the steps before, oldest first) marks an edge infeasible when activating it alone would exceed the limit. The scheduler applies the limit to its whole schedule.

The linear model of a window

An EdgeLinearStep(A, B, edges) is one step: the state transition A_k and the input columns of every candidate edge. By default each edge has one column, its force magnitude in newtons. Edges with more inputs give their column ranges with columns, or the step takes one block per edge. An EdgeLinearModel(steps, projection) is the window, and projection maps the state at its end to the coordinates whose controllability is assessed.

For stacked Cartesian states [r_i; v_i], these helpers build the model:

  • edge_input_matrix(graph, masses) gives the continuous input matrix of the link forces at the graph's geometry. A positive input pushes the ends apart along the line of sight, as the laser links do in a run.
  • link_force_jacobian(graph, masses, forces) gives the state Jacobian of nominal link forces, for a model linearized about a positive force (see Signed and push-only inputs).
  • zoh_discretize(Ac, Bc, dt) holds the inputs over a step.

The dynamics' own Jacobian is the model's to supply. The example examples/constellation_edge_scheduling.jl builds it for point-mass gravity in cartesian_step:

steps = map(zip(midpoints, graphs)) do (t, graph)
    Ac = gravity_jacobian(solution(t))           # your model's Jacobian
    A, B = zoh_discretize(Ac, edge_input_matrix(graph, masses), dt)
    EdgeLinearStep(A, B, graph.pairs)
end
model = EdgeLinearModel(steps, range_output_matrix(solution(t_end), pairs; rate_scale=1 / n))

A model in other coordinates supplies its own A_k and per-edge B_k. Its projection carries its output map to Cartesian coordinates, followed by the Cartesian output: projection = C_out * C_cart. A physical output of the relative motion, δy = C δz_rel, gives projection = C * T[relative_rows, :] with T from barycentric_transform, and the Gramian is then the output Gramian.

What the links cannot reach

Each link force acts equally and oppositely on its two ends along the line of centers. Two conserved quantities follow:

  • The barycenter. The forces sum to zero, so the continuous input matrix B_c has no barycenter component. barycentric_transform separates the barycenter rows from the relative ones.
  • The rigid rotation. The forces also leave the total angular momentum about the planet's center unchanged, and in free space the angular momentum about the barycenter too. So the formation's rigid rotation cannot be driven in free space.

In orbit, unequal gravity couples both weakly to the shape. Over a step held at constant force, the barycenter rows of a discretized B_k are small but not zero, and the rotation about the barycenter can change only by trading angular momentum with the barycenter's orbit, which is slow over a window short against the orbital period.

The relative rows T[relative_rows, :] therefore drop the barycenter but keep the rotation. On them, λ_min measures the weakly reachable rotation rather than the shape, and so does log det with a small ε. For those two metrics, project on a rotation-free output: range_output_matrix(states, pairs) gives the pairwise ranges and range rates, which neither a translation nor a rotation of the whole formation changes. λ₁ and the direction energy can keep the relative rows, since there the user chooses the direction.

Rows in different units spread the Gramian's eigenvalues by the units alone. A projection with ranges in metres and range rates in metres per second does so too, so scale the rates to metres: rate_scale = 1 / n for the mean motion n, or divide velocity rows by n on a relative projection. Then choose ε against the scaled spectrum.

The Gramian and its metrics

reachability_matrix(model; active) returns R_N = P [Φ(k₀+N, k₀+1) B_{k₀} … B_{k₀+N−1}], restricted to the active links, together with a name for each column. controllability_gramian(model; active) returns W_N = R_N R_Nᵀ. With a physical output projection, the column of an activation is that activation's terminal gain: the change in the output at the window's end per unit input.

The metrics are the values of controllability_metric:

  • LargestEigenvalueMetric(): λ₁, the easiest direction. Its eigenvector is the dominant deformation mode.
  • SmallestEigenvalueMetric(): λ_min, the hardest direction.
  • LogDetMetric(epsilon=ε): log det(W + εI), the volume of the reachable set. Directions with eigenvalues below ε count little.
  • DirectionEnergyMetric(d): dᵀ W d, the reach along one chosen deformation.

gramian_metrics(W; epsilon, direction) returns all four at once, with the eigenvalues, the eigenvectors and the dominant mode.

Schedules

schedule_edges(model, strategy; max_active, graphs) returns an EdgeSchedule. It holds the active links of each step, the Gramian of the schedule, the terminals that carry each link, and tied_rounds. The strategies are:

  • ConstantEdgeSet(edges=...): one link set for the whole window, each link active at every step where it is admissible. With metric in place of edges, the set is built from the candidates, one link per round, by that metric.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(LargestEigenvalueMetric()): greedy λ₁.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(LogDetMetric(epsilon=ε)): greedy volume.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(SmallestEigenvalueMetric(); tie_break=LogDetMetric(epsilon=ε)): greedy λ_min. λ_min stays zero until every direction can be reached, so until then the tie break decides.

The greedy schedules add one (step, link) activation at a time over the whole window, and fill every admissible slot. The built-in metrics never fall as links are added, so the metric chooses which links are active, not how many. When no limit binds, every strategy returns the same schedule, with every feasible link active; strategies differ only under a binding max_active, terminal or duty-cycle limit.

Two values count as equal within the roundoff of a symmetric eigensolver, about eps · n · λ_max, carried into each metric. tied_rounds counts the rounds in which every candidate tied on the metric, so that the tie break or the candidate order chose. A greedy λ_min schedule whose tied_rounds equals its number of activations has been chosen by its tie break alone, which is the sign of a projection that keeps a direction no link can reach.

Without graphs, a Boolean feasible matrix (edges by steps) gives the feasible edges. duty_cycle and previous apply a duty cycle across window boundaries.

Minimum input energy

minimum_input_energy(model, schedule, target; initial_state) returns the least Σ |δu|² that moves the projected end state to target. When an initial_state is given, the inputs also correct its free drift. The result holds:

  • the energy, the inputs by step and link, and the residual;
  • whether the target is reachable: a target outside the reachable subspace gets its least-squares inputs and is reported as not reachable;
  • most_negative_input and nonnegative, the sign check described next.

Signed and push-only inputs

The inputs are signed in the sign of the model's columns. With edge_input_matrix, a positive input pushes the ends apart and a negative one pulls them together, which a laser cannot do. Which signed answer is meaningful depends on the nominal force the model is linearized about:

  • About zero link force, as in the example, the inputs are the link forces themselves. A push-only laser then needs u ≥ 0: the target is reachable when R u = y has a nonnegative solution. A rank-deficient Gramian can still allow particular targets; full rank is necessary to reach every output target, but does not by itself guarantee push-only reachability. When nonnegative is false, the energy is that of a two-way actuator, a lower bound on the push-only energy.
  • About a positive nominal force F*, signed perturbations are legitimate within −F* ≤ δu ≤ F_max − F*. The model's A_k must then include the nominal force's own Jacobian, (F*/ρ)(I − ℓ̂ℓ̂ᵀ) on the relative position, which link_force_jacobian adds to Ac.

The Gramian and the energy do not depend on the sign convention of the columns; the returned inputs do.

Limits

  • The greedy schedules carry no optimality guarantee under terminal and duty-cycle limits. log det(W + εI) is monotone and submodular in the activations, and that is the property greedy bounds rest on.
  • Each greedy round evaluates every admissible (step, link) slot, with an eigendecomposition for the eigenvalue metrics. That suits formations of a few spacecraft over tens of steps, not a proliferated constellation.
  • The graph's flags are evaluated at the sample times only. A link that closes or opens between samples is not seen.
  • link_force_jacobian holds the force's magnitude fixed. A beam force that falls with range adds a radial term that it leaves out.

examples/constellation_edge_scheduling.jl runs the pieces end to end on four spacecraft. It compares a constant set and the three greedy schedules by their Gramians on the formation's ranges and range rates, and by the least two-way energy of one reconfiguration, with its sign check.
