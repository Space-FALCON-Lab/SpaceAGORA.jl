What it does:
- `DataBacklogPolicy(; horizon_s)` scores each available directed link by the
  data it could move in the next `horizon_s` seconds, as a rate:
  `min(c, stored_tx / horizon_s, room_rx / horizon_s)` in bit/s, with `c` the
  link's `link_data_rate_bps`. A transmitter without a store counts as always
  holding data, and a receiver without one as always having room.
- `stored_bits(ctx, i)` and `store_room_bits(ctx, i)` read a spacecraft's store
  at the scheduling state, for a policy of your own: NaN and Inf without a
  store.

Rule: the existing policies, their scores and every run that does not use the
new policy are unchanged.

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings in the author's own review. Points a reviewer should check:

- **F1, the stores' tolerances in the score.** A store that has just emptied
  holds a hair of data (a few nanobits in the tests), within its `1e-9`
  tolerance. Read raw, it scored a tiny positive backlog, and the scheduler kept
  selecting a link with nothing to send: the first run test caught it. The
  score counts a store within its empty tolerance as holding nothing, and one
  within its full tolerance as having no room, by the store's own tests through
  two stubs. This is the same shape as I-5b's energy-balancing fix for a full
  battery. The accessors stay raw: `stored_bits` is the `data_stored_bits`
  column's value.
- **F2, ends without a store.** A transmitter without a store has no backlog to
  limit the score, and a receiver without one no room limit, so such a link
  scores its rate, as under `DataThroughputPolicy`. A store whose state lacks
  `data_stored_bits` (a caller's own state) is treated the same.
- **F3, the unit.** bit/s, as `DataThroughputPolicy`, so a `WeightedPolicy` of
  the two needs no rescaling; a test pins the mix.
- **Low, by design: a per-step rule.** The score looks `horizon_s` ahead at the
  scheduling state; it does not plan contacts. The selection changes only at
  accepted steps, as for every policy.

### User-selected ongoing-production score, 2026-10-23

The user explicitly chose “Count ongoing production (recommended)”. A backlog
score is now the minimum of link rate, held transmitter data divided by the
horizon plus its current source production rate, and receiver room divided by
the horizon. It samples the directed transmitter's current state, context and
time; it estimates that rate continuing, without predicting future source
changes or incoming relay traffic. An empty producer remains eligible, while
an empty nonproducer and a full receiver still score zero. Store tolerances and
unlimited endpoints without stores retain their existing treatment.
