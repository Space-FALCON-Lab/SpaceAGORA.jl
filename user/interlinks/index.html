Interlinks

Use this page to connect spacecraft through interlinks, with a policy that selects which links are active.

What to read next:

  • Configuring a Simulation
  • Parallel Execution

Interlinks and constellations

An interlink is a connection between two spacecraft terminals that does something while the geometry allows it: push the spacecraft apart, and in later releases carry power or data. A constellation is a set of spacecraft and their orbits, laid out and run together. They are different things: a constellation needs no interlinks, and two spacecraft can be linked without forming one.

Links and link kinds

Each candidate link has its own kind, a value of a subtype of AbstractInterlink. The kind decides when the link can close and what it does to its two endpoints:

  • link_range_m(link): the maximum separation in metres. Every kind implements it.
  • link_available(link, a, b, ctx): whether the link can close. By default, the endpoints are apart and within link_range_m.
  • link_force(link, a, b, ctx): the inertial forces (N) on a and b while the link is active. By default, none.

a and b are the endpoints' state samples (StateSample: position, velocity, mass, attitude and spacecraft_index), in the order of the link's key: the lower (spacecraft, terminal) pair first, or, for a directed link, the transmitter first. ctx is a LinkContext holding the time (s into the run) and the run's planet.

The built-in kinds are:

  • LaserMomentumLink(; power_w, magnification, range_m): an open-cavity laser link with the idealized central force magnification * power_w / c, directed away from the other endpoint, equal and opposite on the two spacecraft. LaserMomentumLink(terminal) takes the numbers of a LaserTerminal.
  • PowerBeamLink(; power_w, wavelength_m, waist_radius_m, range_m): a power beam from a LaserTerminal to a PowerReceiver. See Power links.

Setting up interlinks

An InterlinkModel holds the candidate links, spacecraft pairs that may never link, and the history of the links the scheduler selected. Endpoints are (spacecraft vector index, terminal number) pairs: the spacecraft's position in dynamics_model.spacecraft, not its id. Each terminal can take part in at most one selected link.

model = InterlinkModel()
for partner in 2:3
    register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, 1), (partner, 1),
        LaserMomentumLink(power_w=10_000.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3))
end
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model,
    scheduling_policy_model=SchedulingPolicyModel(:gve_sma))

Start with examples/gve_sma_interlinks.jl. It builds a three-spacecraft case, registers two candidate links, and selects the link that increases the target's instantaneous semimajor-axis rate. examples/targeted_40sat_sma_interlinks.jl uses the same interface with thirty helpers and ten targets. These are analytical atmosphere examples; they do not require native GRAM or private telemetry.

include("examples/gve_sma_interlinks.jl")
args = gve_sma_configuration(duration=120.0)
solution = run_simulation(args; return_solution=true)
history = solution.prob.p.args.interlink_model.history

Terminals

A spacecraft's terminals are its n_terminal virtual terminals, numbered 1:n_terminal, followed by the terminal hardware mounted on it, in mounting order. A LaserTerminal mounted with add_actuator! is terminal hardware; a kind of your own opts in with SpaceAGORA.link_terminal(::MyTerminal) = true. terminal_count(spacecraft) gives the total, and mounted_link_terminals(spacecraft) lists the hardware with its numbers. A spacecraft with only hardware sets n_terminal=0. Virtual terminals stay for idealized studies, which run as before.

An endpoint's terminal is a number, or the mounted terminal itself:

bus = Link(root=true, m=100.0)
sc = SpacecraftModel(root=bus, links=[bus], inertia_tensor=bus.inertia, n_terminal=0)
terminal = LaserTerminal(boresight=[0.0, 1.0, 0.0], max_power_w=50.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3)
add_actuator!(sc, bus, terminal)
# ... a second spacecraft, `other`, likewise
spacecraft = [sc, other]
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, terminal), (2, 1), LaserMomentumLink(terminal))

A terminal must be able to carry its link (terminal_supports(terminal, link)). A LaserTerminal carries LaserMomentumLink, and a virtual terminal carries any kind. terminal_number(spacecraft, terminal) gives a mounted terminal's number. Two mounted terminals with equal fields cannot be told apart, so for those give the number.

Directed links

A kind with link_directed(link) = true runs one way: from the first endpoint given to register_link! (the transmitter) to the second (the receiver). Its key keeps that order, so its hooks receive the transmitter as a. The opposite direction between the same terminals is a separate link. Laser momentum links are undirected.

Registering many links

register_links!(model, spacecraft, pairs, link) registers one link per spacecraft pair. all_pairs(spacecraft) gives every pair, or you can pass any vector of (i, j) index pairs. For a constellation laid out by build_constellation or walker_constellation (see Constellations), its planes and slots give the usual topologies:

  • same_plane_pairs(spacecraft): every pair within each plane.
  • ring_pairs(spacecraft): each slot with the next in its plane, closing the ring.
  • grid_pairs(spacecraft; wrap_planes=true): the "+grid", the rings plus each slot with the same slot of the next plane. Pass wrap_planes=false for a Walker star, whose first and last planes counter-rotate. link is a kind value, or a function

(i, j) -> link. Each spacecraft's links take its terminals that can carry them in turn, starting again from the first when they run out; links that share a terminal compete for it when the scheduler selects. Pass terminals=(i, j) -> (terminal_i, terminal_j) to choose the terminals yourself.

register_links!(model, spacecraft, all_pairs(spacecraft), LaserMomentumLink(power_w=40.0))
# or, for a constellation's +grid:
register_links!(model, spacecraft, grid_pairs(spacecraft), LaserMomentumLink(power_w=40.0))

Terminal hardware and power

A hardware terminal that is a link endpoint is driven by its links for the whole run:

  • Its level. While one of its links is active, it runs at the level the link sets, link_terminal_levels(link). For a LaserMomentumLink, that is the link's power_w at both ends. Otherwise its level is 0.
  • Its power. It draws actuator_consumption(terminal, level) from its spacecraft's power budget: power_w / efficiency for a LaserTerminal. The draw shows in the power columns, and in the actuator_level and actuator_power columns.
  • Its force. Only the link's force applies while the link is active. The terminal's own recoil applies only when a controller drives a terminal that is no link's endpoint.
  • Controllers. A mounted-actuation controller's levels for a link-driven terminal apply nothing, and setup says so once.
  • Limits. A link that would drive its terminal past its limits (above a LaserTerminal's max_power_w) is refused at setup. Build the link from the terminal, LaserMomentumLink(terminal), to match them.

When the battery sheds tier 1 (see Powering a Spacecraft), it sheds laser terminals. Links through shed hardware terminals then become unavailable, and stay so until the tier clears: a shed terminal keeps asking for its link's power, so the tier clears when the battery can supply it again. Virtual terminals draw nothing and are never shed.

Power links

A PowerBeamLink carries power, one way, from a LaserTerminal (the transmitter) to a PowerReceiver (the receiver). It applies no force.

receiver = PowerReceiver(normal=[0.0, -1.0, 0.0], radius_m=0.25, efficiency=0.4)
add_actuator!(other, other.root, receiver)
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, terminal), (2, receiver),
    PowerBeamLink(power_w=80.0, wavelength_m=1.064e-6, waist_radius_m=0.05, range_m=50e3))
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model,
    scheduling_policy_model=SchedulingPolicyModel(:power_delivery))
  • The beam. While the link is active, the transmitter runs at power_w optical watts. It draws power_w / efficiency from its own battery as a tier-1 laser load (see Terminal hardware and power). The beam is Gaussian:
    • its waist, of 1/e² radius waist_radius_m, is at the transmitter;
    • its spot radius at range d, beam_spot_radius_m(link, d), is w0 √(1 + (d / z_R)²), with the Rayleigh range z_R = π w0² / λ.
  • The receiver. It captures beam_capture_fraction(link, radius_m, d), 1 − exp(−2 r² / w²), of the beam through its aperture. It delivers that times its efficiency to its bus, up to its max_power_w (link_received_power_w).
    • For example, with a 1.064 µm beam from a 5 cm waist, a 25 cm receiver catches nearly all of it out to about 10 km, about a quarter at 100 km, and under 1 % at 1000 km.
  • Generation. The delivered power is generation in the receiver's power budget. It shows in power_generated_w and in its own power_received_w column, charges the receiver's battery, and helps an empty battery recover. A full receiver battery curtails it, as it does solar power; the transmitter keeps paying.
  • Availability. The link can close within range_m, while the segment between the spacecraft clears the planet (a sphere of its equatorial radius). When the transmitter's battery sheds tier 1, the beam closes until the tier clears.
  • Direction. A PowerReceiver can only receive, and a LaserTerminal can only transmit a power beam (terminal_supports(terminal, link, side)).
  • Scheduling. Policies that score links by their forces never select a power beam, so use :power_delivery, which scores each link by the power it would deliver. A run uses one policy; mixing momentum and power links waits for a later scheduling contract.
  • Columns. A run with a power beam adds power_received_w and power_beamed_w per spacecraft to the power columns.
  • Not yet modelled. Pointing and jitter, the receiver's incidence angle, the atmosphere, beam quality, and the beam's radiation pressure are not modelled.

Scheduling

At the start of the run and after every accepted solver step, the engine marks a candidate available when its spacecraft pair is not forbidden, both spacecraft are active, neither terminal is shed, and its link's link_available allows it. The scheduling policy then scores the available links by the forces their link_force gives, and selects the terminal matching with the largest total score. Only links with a positive score can be selected. The selected schedule is held during trial stages, which avoids changing it during rejected steps. An inactive endpoint contributes no force even if an earlier schedule still names its link.

The saved laser_dv vector integrates the interlink acceleration. laser_delta_sma integrates its contribution to the instantaneous osculating semimajor-axis rate; it is not a comparison against a separately propagated unforced trajectory. The history belongs to the returned run configuration. With the default state isolation, the input configuration is unchanged.

Optional matching backend

Tree-shaped components are solved in Julia. Cyclic components use NetworkX's maximum-weight matching through the optional PythonCall extension. Core loading and tree matching do not initialize Python. A cyclic component without the backend produces an installation error rather than using an approximate match.

Install PythonCall in the Julia environment used for the run, and install NetworkX (>=3.4,<4) in a compatible Python environment. Set JULIA_PYTHONCALL_EXE to that interpreter before starting Julia; when managing Python yourself, also set JULIA_CONDAPKG_BACKEND=Null. Then explicitly load PythonCall and SpaceAGORA in either order. For example, in a separate Julia project containing SpaceAGORA:

using Pkg
Pkg.add("PythonCall")
using SpaceAGORA, PythonCall

The backend requires Python's global interpreter lock. Run cyclic matching on its owning thread, or use independent process workers. Merely adding more Julia threads does not make arbitrary calls into Python safe. Both matching paths use exact integer weights derived from the floating-point scores. Equal-score ties may choose different valid optima across backend versions.

A custom link kind

A kind implements link_range_m and the effects it has. This one pulls its endpoints together with a constant force:

struct PullLink <: SpaceAGORA.AbstractInterlink
    force_n::Float64
    range_m::Float64
end
SpaceAGORA.link_range_m(link::PullLink) = link.range_m
function SpaceAGORA.link_force(link::PullLink, a, b, ctx)
    direction = (b.pos_ii - a.pos_ii) / sqrt(sum(abs2, b.pos_ii - a.pos_ii))
    return (link.force_n * direction, -link.force_n * direction)
end

register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, 1), (2, 1), PullLink(3.0, 2_000.0))

A run refuses at setup a link whose kind has no link_range_m method. Links of different kinds can be registered in one model.

Supported scope

Interlinks currently apply a translational force to rigid spacecraft. The laser momentum link does not model terminal pointing, occultation, beam losses, torques or thermal dynamics. Links through hardware terminals draw battery power and close when it is shed; links between virtual terminals draw none. Actuator faults on a link-driven terminal do not yet act on its link. The spacecraft's battery_energy_index and tempurature_index fields are metadata, not availability constraints. The cavity magnification and force law are modeling assumptions; passing implementation tests does not validate a physical laser system.

Use a full-state solver such as :tsit5. Split solvers, external propagator coupling, articulated/compliant vehicles and independent constellation ensembles are rejected for linked spacecraft. Near circularity, :gve_eccentricity also rejects candidate sets that can simultaneously apply several positive-score links to one target: individual eccentricity-rate norms are not additive there. The semimajor-axis policies do not have that restriction.

Scheduling follows accepted steps, so changes of range or preferred link are resolved at that cadence. Check timestep convergence for a new case. A checkpoint resume must retain the interlink topology, link kinds (each named by its module and type, with its concrete type parameters) and parameters, scoring policy, and each spacecraft's terminal hardware: its kinds, in mounting order, and every field of each, such as a receiver's aperture or a transmitter's efficiency; a new run is required when those change. Checkpoints written by SpaceAGORA 0.4 and earlier still resume. The resumed history covers the resumed interval, while integrated diagnostic states continue from the checkpoint.

Deprecated names

The names from when every link was a laser link keep working until SpaceAGORA 1.0. The old keywords, fields and functions warn once per process, and throw under julia --depwarn=error. The two old type names are aliases of the new types and cannot warn on their own: InterLinkParameters(), InterLinkParameters(terminal), the positional InterLinkParameters(P, B, range) and ConstellationInterLinkModel(; forbidden_pairs) run silently, so search for those names when you move to the new ones.
