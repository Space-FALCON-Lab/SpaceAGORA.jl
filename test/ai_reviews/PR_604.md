## Changed Files

- `src/models/dynamics/multibody/compliant/compliant_multibody.jl`: owns the
  shared midpoint damping wrench, standalone diagnostics and compiled joint
  accumulation. Parent and child damping transfer couples have the same sign;
  rotational compliance and actuator couples retain their opposite signs.
- `src/models/dynamics/multibody/compliant/compliant_robot_arm_dynamics.jl`:
  imports that internal helper and transfers the complete wrench to either the
  spacecraft mount or the preceding arm body. Frame conversions remain at the
  existing COM-load application sites.

The public model and diagnostic types keep their fields. The user guide and
docstrings explain the corrected compliance-torque values, which now include
the damping transfer couples about the anchors.

## Findings

The old force pair left moment `-delta_r × C*delta_v` inside a nominally isolated
joint and damped common rigid rotation when anchors were separated. The new
midpoint slip is the difference between the rigid velocity fields at one shared
point. Transferring equal-and-opposite forces from that point to the two
anchors yields zero total moment and power `-s' C s` for the damper.

Checked the standalone, compiled attachment, fixed/moving mount and coupled
robot-arm consumers. All apply the added couples once. The existing body-frame
torque conversion and gyroscopic terms remain unchanged. The helper creates
no mutable state; existing allocation regressions pass.

## Residual Risk

Trajectories with separated rotating anchors intentionally change because the
old damper was not objective or angular-momentum conserving. Consumers must
not assume the two diagnostic compliance torque fields are negatives without
including force moment arms. Non-positive-semidefinite damping matrices are
not claimed to dissipate energy. Translational spring and finite-angle
rotational spring laws are unchanged; arbitrary anisotropic stiffness still
needs its own frame/energy qualification. No claim of MuJoCo coupling support
follows from correcting the core joint law.
