Energy Depletion Guidance for aerobraking

Energy Depletion Guidance (EDG) controls the drag produced during an aerobraking passage by changing the angle of selected spacecraft panels. A larger exposed area generally removes orbital energy more quickly, but it also changes aerodynamic loading and heating. EDG chooses panel commands within that tradeoff: it can seek the greatest energy depletion allowed by its configured constraints, or seek an orbital target that lies within its predicted reachable range.

The original EDG formulation was developed for atmospheric passes with articulating solar panels by Falcone and Putnam (2022). The generalized formulation of Simha, Yu, and Falcone (2026) extends the treatment to multiple planetary bodies, structural constraints, and targeting. These papers establish the scientific context. This guide describes the implementation currently shipped in SpaceAGORA, including the places where a software setting, a prediction, and an accepted physical result must be distinguished.

EDG is currently part of SpaceAGORA itself. There is no separate EDG package to install. Its proposed future adapter boundary is documented in the EDG ownership and lifecycle plan; that plan does not change the configuration described here.

What the guidance and controller do

The implementation separates two decisions. Guidance selects the mode and, when targeting is enabled, evaluates a reachable energy bracket. Control uses that decision to obtain a panel angle, applies the active heat-rate and structural restrictions, and updates the selected panel geometry at each control callback. Aerodynamic and thermal models then evaluate the resulting geometry during propagation.

The two public model types are AerobrakingEnergyDepletionGuidanceModel and AerobrakingEnergyDepletionControlModel. They share an immutable AerobrakingEnergyDepletionConfig and the same mutable AerobrakingEnergyDepletionState. Sharing the state is essential: a controller with a separate, initially identical state does not receive the guidance model's decisions. Simulation preflight checks this pairing and requires one state entry per spacecraft.

In :max_energy_depletion mode, the controller begins from the configured maximum angle. The selected submodes can reduce that command to satisfy the internal heat-rate or structural model. Heat-load control can also schedule a low-drag interval within the passage. The name describes the objective of this policy; it is not a claim that every propagated case is a globally optimal trajectory.

In :targeting mode, guidance first predicts a range of exit energies and apoapsides using low-drag and maximum-depletion trajectories. A target within that range can proceed to a switch-time calculation: the controller applies the admissible high-drag command before the switch and the minimum angle after it. The switch times stored in the state are elapsed simulation times in seconds, rather than durations measured from the latest callback.

Targeting also checks a sampled sequence of candidate switches for finite predictions, heat-load feasibility, and the expected energy ordering. It uses a contiguous acceptable portion of that sequence; at least two acceptable samples are required. A failed check can therefore mean that the sampled prediction did not establish an admissible bracket. It does not prove that no physically feasible trajectory exists. If targeting cannot proceed, the model falls back to maximum depletion when that mode is also enabled, or to :safe_low_drag when targeting is the only configured mode. The latter name means that the minimum panel angle is commanded; it does not certify that all physical limits can be met.

Energy, heating, and loading

The energy used for orbital diagnostics is the planet-relative two-body specific orbital energy,

\[\varepsilon = \frac{\lVert\mathbf{v}_{\mathrm{i}}\rVert^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{\lVert\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{i}}\rVert},\]

where position and velocity are expressed in the inertial frame and the result is in J/kg. For a bound Keplerian ellipse, $\varepsilon=-\mu/(r_a+r_p)$. Consequently, target_apoapsis_radius_m is an apoapsis radius from the planet's centre, in metres, rather than an altitude. The current target mapping uses the predicted periapsis variation across the reachable bracket; a fixed periapsis should not be assumed when translating a desired apoapsis into energy. With additional perturbations enabled, this two-body energy remains a useful diagnostic, but its change should not automatically be attributed entirely to atmospheric drag.

Atmospheric loading uses air-relative velocity in the rotating planet frame. The environment query accounts for local wind when wind is enabled, so this speed generally differs from the inertial speed in the energy expression. Dynamic pressure is $q_{\mathrm{dyn}}=\rho V_{\mathrm{air}}^2/2$, in Pa.

Heat rate and heat load are different quantities. EDG's heat-rate limit is in W/cm², while its heat-load limit is in J/cm². Heat load is the time integral of heat rate on a link; it is not the instantaneous heat rate at the current state. The conversion from either square-centimetre unit to its square-metre counterpart introduces a factor of $10^4$.

The controller uses an internal Maxwellian heat-rate estimate to choose an angle. That estimate takes the wall temperature to equal the sampled atmospheric temperature. The propagated thermal model evaluates the actual configured thermal settings and executed link geometry. Its saved heat rates can therefore differ from last_heat_rate_w_cm2, which records the controller's estimate. Assess a thermal constraint using the propagated panel quantities as well as the guidance diagnostics.

In the current implementation, the heat-load input is cumulative. The controller reads the largest accumulated heat load among its controlled links. If heat-load control is enabled and that value reaches heat_load_limit_j_cm2, the command is reduced to the minimum angle. Passage resets do not subtract an entry heat-load baseline or clear the propagated heat state. A finite limit in a multiple-passage run must therefore be interpreted with this cumulative behaviour in mind; it is not automatically a renewed budget for each passage.

The structural setting also needs a precise interpretation. The controller forms a drag area from the sum of each link's reference area multiplied by its internal free-molecular drag coefficient. Let that sum at a candidate angle be $A_D(\alpha)$. The pressure-valued setting defines the modeled force bound

\[D(\alpha) = q_{\mathrm{dyn}} A_D(\alpha) \;\leq\; D_{\mathrm{limit}} = p_{\mathrm{limit}} A_D(\alpha_{\max}).\]

Controlled panels use the candidate angle in this calculation; other links retain their modeled angles within the configured bounds. Thus structural_load_limit_pa is a drag-based load proxy normalized by a reference drag area. It is neither a finite-element stress limit nor a direct upper bound on ambient dynamic pressure. Its connection to a spacecraft's hardware limits must be established for that spacecraft.

If even the minimum angle exceeds an internal heat-rate or structural bound, the corresponding control calculation returns the minimum angle. The simulation is not terminated merely by that fallback. Commands are also held between control callbacks, so checking a constraint only at those callbacks is insufficient to establish its maximum over the passage.

Configure a paired EDG model

The following constructs a paired model for a spacecraft with controllable panels at link indices 2 and 3. It deliberately follows the simple panel-cap exercise used in the Odyssey tutorial: the infinite heat-rate threshold leaves the cap unmodified. It is a configuration example, not a thermal protection policy.

using SpaceAGORA

edg_config = AerobrakingEnergyDepletionConfig(
    guidance_modes = (:max_energy_depletion,),
    max_energy_submodes = (:heat_rate,),
    controlled_panel_links = (2, 3),
    max_alpha_rad = deg2rad(30.0),
    min_alpha_rad = 1e-4,
    heat_rate_limit_w_cm2 = Inf,
)

edg_state = AerobrakingEnergyDepletionState(num_sats = 1)
edg_guidance = AerobrakingEnergyDepletionGuidanceModel(edg_config, edg_state)
edg_control = AerobrakingEnergyDepletionControlModel(edg_config, edg_state)

guidance_model = GuidanceModel(
    guidance_effectors = (edg_guidance,),
    guidance_rates = [3.0],
)
control_model = ControlModel(
    control_effectors = (edg_control,),
    control_rates = [0.1],
)

Pass these guidance_model and control_model values to the SimulationConfiguration constructor along with the mission, environment, dynamics, and initial-time settings for the case. Set num_sats to the number of spacecraft in that configuration. The EDG state is indexed by spacecraft position in the configuration, while controlled_panel_links indexes the links within each spacecraft. Every selected link must exist and must be a rotatable, non-root link. On a spacecraft with articulated dynamics, this kinematic panel control is restricted to :fixed links belonging to the root body. A panel on a moving joint body is refused; EDG does not provide a joint motor for that body.

Despite their names, guidance_rates and control_rates contain callback periods in seconds. The example runs guidance every 3 seconds and control every 0.1 seconds. Choose these periods together with the integration and output resolution needed to resolve the passage. A smaller period is not, by itself, evidence of a more accurate prediction or an accepted constraint margin.

The default control effector uses the same panel selection as the EDG configuration. Keep the prediction and actuation selections aligned unless a different arrangement has been deliberately modeled and checked. For fixed-attitude runs, the force and thermal models must also respond to the changed panel geometry. The Odyssey example uses AerodynamicCoefficientfM(fixed_attitude_incidence=:attitude) for this purpose; see panel angles and heating. The EDG effector directly updates panel geometry. It does not add actuator rate limits, hinge dynamics, or a spacecraft attitude controller.

The principal settings are:

SettingMeaning
guidance_modes(:max_energy_depletion,), (:targeting,), or both. Including maximum depletion makes it available as the targeting fallback.
max_energy_submodesA nonempty selection from :heat_rate, :structural_load, and :heat_load. These select the restrictions considered by the EDG calculations.
min_alpha_rad, max_alpha_radCommand bounds in radians. Both must be finite, with 0 ≤ min_alpha_rad ≤ max_alpha_rad.
heat_rate_limit_w_cm2Internal heat-rate threshold in W/cm². Use a physically justified positive value when heat-rate protection is intended.
heat_load_limit_j_cm2Cumulative heat-load threshold in J/cm² under the current implementation.
structural_load_limit_paPressure-valued parameter defining the drag-based force bound described above.
target_apoapsis_radius_mDesired apoapsis radius in metres. Supply a finite positive radius when enabling targeting.
heat_load_switch_solver:closed_form or :tpbvp_integration for the heat-load switching calculation. Selecting a solver does not establish physical validity for a new atmosphere or vehicle.
planning_horizon_sHorizon used by the prediction machinery, not the mission duration.
targeting_certification_samplesNumber of candidate switch samples, at least two; the default is nine.
targeting_energy_order_tolerance_jkgTolerance for the candidate energy-ordering check, in J/kg.
targeting_heat_load_tolerance_j_cm2Tolerance for the candidate heat-load check, in J/cm².

The physical limits default to Inf. A selected submode with an infinite limit does not impose a finite protection threshold. Heat-rate and structural restrictions require positive finite limits; zero does not request zero heating or zero loading. Configure limits from the vehicle and mission requirements, and validate them against the propagated quantities. The stored switch_recompute_interval_s field is currently inactive: changing it does not introduce periodic replanning.

For a first complete run, use the Odyssey surrogate aerobraking tutorial. From the repository root, its independent example environment is prepared and run with:

julia --project=examples/odyssey_surrogate_env examples/odyssey_surrogate_env/setup.jl
julia --project=examples/odyssey_surrogate_env examples/odyssey_surrogate.jl

The setup obtains the identified atmosphere and planetary assets required by the example. It uses a frozen Mars atmosphere grid and does not require a native GRAM installation. The default comparison changes the panel cap from 90° to 30° and records the resulting trajectories and heating. It does not exercise targeting, a finite thermal threshold, or a stochastic GRAM campaign. Those distinctions make it a useful installation and geometry exercise without treating it as validation of every EDG mode.

Read the result and the guidance state

Use run_simulation(args; return_results=true) to retain the results table and the configuration that actually ran. Under the default isolate_state=true, SpaceAGORA deep-copies the input configuration, including the shared guidance/control state. Consequently, the caller's original edg_state is not the place to read the completed run's diagnostics.

For a configuration containing the paired guidance model above:

result = run_simulation(args; return_results = true)
run_guidance = only(filter(
    model -> model isa AerobrakingEnergyDepletionGuidanceModel,
    result.configuration.guidance_model.guidance_effectors,
))
state = run_guidance.state

state.selected_mode[1]
state.last_alpha_rad[1]
state.last_heat_load_j_cm2[1]

result.table contains the configured saved quantities; result.files lists files written by the run. Passing return_solution=true as well retains the ODE solution in result.solution. The EDG state itself holds current or last-update diagnostics, not a complete time history:

State fieldsInterpretation
selected_mode, targeting_active, safe_low_dragCurrent mode and fallback status. A passage reset can return the mode to :inactive.
target_energy_jkg, bracket_min_energy_jkg, bracket_max_energy_jkgTarget and predicted bracket energies in J/kg. Interpret them with the mode and bracketing flags.
energy_bracketing_evaluated, energy_bracketing_countWhether the current passage has been bracketed, and the cumulative number of bracketing evaluations in the run.
targeting_switch_s, heat_load_switches_sCached switch times in elapsed simulation seconds. Infinite values are sentinels, not finite scheduled events.
heat_load_switch_solved, last_switch_solve_tBookkeeping for the heat-load solve and the latest switch calculation. A recorded calculation is not an independent feasibility certificate.
last_alpha_rad, last_alpha_heat_rate_rad, last_alpha_structural_radApplied command and the most recently computed angle bounds, in radians.
last_heat_rate_w_cm2, last_heat_load_j_cm2, last_dynamic_pressure_paInternal heat-rate estimate, controlled-link cumulative heat-load input, and sampled dynamic pressure.

For a passage analysis, save the command, mode, switch diagnostics, panel heat rates and heat loads alongside position, velocity, density, and time. Use SaveField getters to retain custom diagnostic quantities; the outputs guide describes the results and trajectory recorder, and the Odyssey example demonstrates recording the executed panel geometry. Saving that geometry matters: recomputing an old state using the panels' final orientation does not reconstruct the forces or heat rates that were applied at that time.

A whole-spacecraft maximum may include the bus, whereas the EDG heat-load input covers its selected links. Label the panel set and aggregation used in each plot or reported margin. Also distinguish a predicted exit energy from the energy measured at the propagated exit event, and an internal heat estimate from the thermal model's output. These comparisons are what reveal prediction error and unmet constraints.

State between passages and repeated runs

A fresh run_simulation initializes EDG state, including the counters. Reusing a configuration therefore starts a fresh EDG run. Default state isolation preserves the guidance/control pairing in the copied configuration. With isolate_state=false, EDG still initializes its state, but the caller retains responsibility for the configuration's other mutable objects.

Atmospheric entry and exit invalidate the affected spacecraft's cached bracket, mode flags, and switch times. Repeated notification of the same crossing does not erase a newly established plan, and ordinary callbacks within a passage retain their cache. Bracketing counts accumulate across passages within a run. Last-command telemetry remains until the next update; neither physical heat loads nor panel geometry are cleared by the cache reset. The next control callback can change the between-passage command according to the configured mode's default.

Passage classification deserves care near the entry interface. The engine's crossing events use a spherical radius, while the EDG in-passage query uses geodetic altitude. At an exact-boundary start, the engine classifies outward motion as outside and inward or tangential motion as inside, within its floating-point boundary band. A known tangential-start case that subsequently rises can retain its inside flag and cached plan through the coast. The Odyssey lifecycle discussion records this limit and the associated solver-phase behaviour.

Maximum-depletion control can run without a paired guidance model, but targeting requires guidance to establish its bracket. A configuration may contain at most one EDG guidance model and one EDG controller. EDG checkpoint writing and resume are currently refused because the checkpoint format does not preserve its passage history. Save analysis outputs normally, but do not plan a restart workflow around an EDG checkpoint.

Scope of the current implementation

Successful execution establishes that a configured calculation ran. It does not, by itself, establish target accuracy, constraint satisfaction, or agreement with a published study. The heat-rate estimate, trajectory prediction, atmosphere representation, held control commands, and propagated vehicle model all contribute to the gap between a planned switch and its achieved result.

In particular, the current prediction paths do not all sample the atmosphere in the same way. The targeting trajectory uses position-dependent queries and wind, whereas an altitude-only prediction helper samples at zero latitude and longitude and omits wind. A regional atmosphere grid that is adequate for the propagated trajectory is not necessarily adequate for that helper. Select an atmosphere whose domain and meaning cover the prediction path being exercised, and compare the predicted passage with the propagated one before interpreting its margins.

Experimental changes to passage heat accounting, targeting, terminal events, and constraint treatment are being evaluated separately. The ongoing GRAM comparison and the experimental EDG work in PR #251 are not presented here as scientifically accepted results. This guide reports the current software contract and published methodological background. A publication or mission study should additionally retain the exact source revision, atmosphere and vehicle data, callback periods, solver settings, constraint definitions, and predicted-versus-propagated diagnostics used for its conclusions.
