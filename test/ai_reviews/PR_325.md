# PR325 review: retired actuator counts, wheel and rod power, hinge-driven energy-depletion panels, and mounted burns

## Scope

Review of the SpaceAGORA `actuator-followups` branch against main after
PR #322 (merge commit a87974858). The PR closes the four items the PI asked to
work on on 2026-10-15 after the actuator gaps: the stored thruster and wheel
counts and the link's thruster Jacobian, power models for reaction wheels and
torque rods, energy-depletion panels through the hinge kind, and the mounted
burn scheduler. One commit per item, then a docs commit.

Authorship disclosure: this record was drafted by the author of the PR. The
plan assigns behaviour PRs of the actuator layer an independent reviewer (the
PI, or the second reviewer when available); this record does not replace that
review.

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings in the change. Points a reviewer should check:

- Low, pre-existing, not changed here: the ideal burn tests its window
  against the evaluation time, closed at both ends, so the derivative
  evaluated at the stop instant, which the solver reuses as the first stage of
  the next step, still thrusts. In the test scenario (1 N for 100 s, then one
  50 s step) the ideal burn uses 0.7 % more propellant than 1 N for 100 s. The
  mounted burn switches through a flag flipped between steps and matches to
  the solver tolerance. The ideal burn moves onto the same flag in a separate
  PR, because that changes existing results.
- Low, by design: the compatibility forms accept the counts and the Jacobian
  silently; a caller that passed meaningful values gets no notice (the values
  never had an effect).
- Fixed after the record (535078b41): the window-edge callback called
  `derivative_discontinuity!(integrator, false)` after switching the engine,
  which declares that nothing changed and keeps the solver's cached
  derivative; a discrete callback counts as a discontinuity unless it clears
  the flag. The callback now leaves it set. The tests passed before because
  other callbacks in the same steps triggered the re-evaluation; after the fix
  the contract tests pass (238 of 238).
- Low, by design: the mounted burn points the engine only while a burn is
  planned or running and only with mounted wheels; it does not desaturate the
  wheels, ignores the gyroscopic term, and clamps each wheel to its torque
  limit after the least-norm split, which can turn the torque at saturation.
- Low, by design: the engine's level is the scheduler's thrust and its
  propellant follows the engine's own specific impulse, while the schedule's
  duration follows the scheduler's thrust and specific impulse; the docstring
  asks for matching values.
- Low, by design: an engine with firing dynamics schedules its thrust at the
  ticks, so a burn that starts between ticks fires from the next tick.
- Low, by design: energy-depletion guidance predicts with the commanded panel
  angles; a slow hinge makes the predictions optimistic (documented).
- Low, modelling: the wheel speed in `wheel_power` is the stored momentum
  over the spin inertia; without regeneration, braking costs nothing beyond
  the base power.
- Low: the visualization `thruster_level` columns now also cover a mounted
  effector that sizes its levels only at its first tick.
