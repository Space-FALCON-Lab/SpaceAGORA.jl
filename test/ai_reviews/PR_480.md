What it does:
- `EnergyBalancingPolicy`: a power beam scores `P × (s_tx − s_rx)` (W), signed,
  0 without a battery at either end.
- `state_of_charge(ctx, spacecraft_index)`: a battery's state of charge at the
  scheduling state, NaN without one.
- `WeightedPolicy(PolicyTerm(policy; scale, weight)...)`: `Σ weight × (score /
  scale)` in term order, with each term's checks; nested mixes.
- The checkpoint entry of a mix: every term's policy, scale and weight.

Rule: runs that use neither policy are bitwise unchanged.

- **Energy balancing.** `candidate_score` returns 0 in these cases: without the
  run's spacecraft models, for an undirected link, for a receiving end that is
  not terminal hardware, and when either state of charge is NaN (no battery).
  Otherwise it returns `link_received_power_w × (s_tx − s_rx)`, signed. Only
  positive scores are selectable, so a beam into a full battery, or from an
  emptier battery to a fuller one, is never chosen. A sender without a battery
  scores 0 rather than counting as full; the PR names this choice and the
  mix that overrides it.
- **State of charge.** `state_of_charge` reads `battery_wh` from the raw state
  view (D-I5-9) over the battery's `capacity_wh`. It reaches the capacity
  through a stub in `InterLinkModels` with the `SpacecraftModel` method in
  `InterlinkTerminals`, the pattern `_terminal_device` already uses. The
  denominator is safe: a battery's constructor refuses a capacity that is not
  finite and positive (`power_components.jl:71`).
- **Weighted mixes.** `PolicyTerm` refuses a non-finite or non-positive `scale`
  and a non-finite `weight`. `WeightedPolicy` refuses an empty mix. A mix sums
  `weight × score / scale` in term order from 0.0, refuses a non-finite term,
  and forwards each term's preparation, cross-candidate checks and setup check.
  So an eccentricity term keeps its near-circular refusal, and a term whose
  policy lacks `candidate_score` is refused at setup.
- **Checkpoints.** A `PolicyTerm` is recorded as its own identity: its policy,
  and `scale` and `weight` with their bits. So a mix that differs in any term,
  scale or weight is refused on resume.
- **History.** One commit on #478's `6049677c6`, with no trailer. It merges
  cleanly with main and with the open #473 to #477 and #479. It opens as a draft
  stacked on #478 (base `typed-scheduling-policies`).

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings in the author's own review. Points a reviewer should check:

- **Bitwise identity.** The new code is reached only through the two new
  policies. `state_of_charge` and the capacity lookup are called only by
  `EnergyBalancingPolicy`. The checkpoint method is reached only by a mix's
  terms. Existing policies, the matching and the checkpoint entry of every
  other policy are unchanged.
- **The score's sign.** Energy balancing is signed: a beam from an emptier
  battery to a fuller one scores negative. Alone, it is never selected
  (positive-score rule); in a mix, it counts against the beam. A full receiver
  counts as 1 (by the power system's own full test, below), so it gives
  `s_tx − 1 ≤ 0` and a beam into a full battery never scores, which is D-I3-5's
  "don't beam into a full battery".
- **No battery.** A link with no battery at either end scores 0, since there is
  no state of charge to balance. A battery-less sender is not treated as full.
- **Term order.** A mix sums its terms in order from 0.0, which a test pins with
  three terms whose sum rounds differently in another order.
- **Mix checks.** An eccentricity term's near-circular refusal runs over the
  mix's scores, so it refuses a near-circular target that two positive-score
  links of the mix could reach. The setup check of `candidate_score` reaches
  every term, nested ones included.
- **2026-10-21, in-house review of 00a0d42fd, P2 (fixed in 95a4aad6d): a beam
  into a full battery was selected when the sender was full too.** The power
  system flags a battery full within `1e-9` of its capacity
  (`battery_boundary!`, and the power callbacks at the start), and its full
  event leaves the stored energy just below the capacity. The policy compared
  the raw states of charge, so a full receiver sat a hair under 1, and a sender
  at 1 (or fuller by more than that hair) scored positive. The review
  reproduced it in a run: a sender kept full by its solar array beamed into a
  receiver flagged full at every accepted step after it filled, as
  `PowerDeliveryPolicy` does, with the receiver curtailing and the sender
  paying. Now a state of charge within the same tolerance counts as 1, so a
  full receiver never scores and two full batteries tie at 0. The review's
  optional point is in too: a link that would deliver no power, or a negative
  power from a link kind of your own, scores 0, rather than a signed zero or a
  positive score from an emptier battery to a fuller one. Tests: a full sender
  against receivers at `1 − 1e-12` and `1 − 1e-10`, and two batteries inside
  the tolerance with the sender a hair fuller, all score 0 and select nothing
  (`PowerDeliveryPolicy` still beams, the negative control); just outside the
  tolerance the beams score; zero and negative delivered power score exactly
  0; and the review's run, where the beam is never selected again once the
  receiver is full, while `PowerDeliveryPolicy` keeps it.
- **2026-10-21, in-house review, P3-5 (fixed in 95a4aad6d).** The
  `AbstractSchedulingPolicy` docstring and the unknown-symbol refusal listed
  four policies; they list six. `link_received_power_w`'s registry `called`
  cell and its docstring name `EnergyBalancingPolicy`.
- **2026-10-21, in-house recheck of 6a2dedcf4: Clear, two P3 points for this
  PR, addressed in 0dbe9bdd3.**
  - P3-A, the threshold. The policy compared the state of charge with
    `1 − 1e-9`, while the power system compares energies, in three
    expressions (`battery_boundary!`, the start of a run, and the step-end
    clear). At the threshold they disagreed by an ulp: the review reproduced a
    receiver started at `initial_soc = 1 − 1e-9` on a 0.033450261609188466 Wh
    battery, flagged full from the start, into which energy balancing beamed at
    every accepted step. One function, `PowerSystems.within_full_tolerance`
    (energy at least `capacity_wh × (1 − 1e-9)`), now decides fullness for
    all three power-system sites and for the policy. The start of a run and the
    step-end clear already used that expression; the boundary event used
    `capacity_wh − 1e-9 × capacity_wh`, which can differ by an ulp, so its
    decision moves by at most an ulp at the threshold. The power and interlink
    runs are bitwise identical (Evidence). Tests: around the threshold (±4 ulps,
    three capacities), a beam scores exactly when `battery_boundary!` does not
    flag the receiver full; and the review's run started at the threshold never
    beams into the receiver, while `PowerDeliveryPolicy` does.
  - P3-D, the text: the tolerance was in three places in the power system, not
    two; it is now in one, which the Residual Risk says.
- **Low, by design: fixed scales.** The scales are the user's, in each term's
  unit; a badly chosen scale lets one term dominate. Normalizing by each step's
  best candidate was decided against (D-I5-5): it would make a link's score
  depend on the other candidates.
- **Low, by design: accepted-step resolution.** The selection changes only at
  accepted steps, as for every policy, so a beam runs to a step's end after the
  states of charge cross.

## Residual Risk

- **Scales are the user's choice.** A mix weighs terms by fixed scales in their
  units. A scale far from the term's typical values makes that term dominate or
  vanish. The interlinks page shows the units.
- **The full test is the power system's.** One function,
  `PowerSystems.within_full_tolerance`, decides a battery's fullness for the
  power system's flags and for energy balancing, so they agree. A receiver just
  below the threshold still scores its small gap. The empty test is still
  written in two places, the boundary event and the start of a run.
- **Energy balancing is a per-step rule.** It weighs delivered power by the
  state-of-charge gap at each accepted step. It does not plan ahead, account
  for the sender's own draw or capacity, or keep a sender reserve. Shedding
  still protects the sender's battery.
- **Accepted-step resolution.** A beam runs to a step's end after the states of
  charge cross.
- **Stacked.** This PR waits for #478. Its diff against main includes #478's
  files until then.
