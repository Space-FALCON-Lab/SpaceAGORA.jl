Edge Scheduling API

This page documents the supported public interface available from SpaceAGORA.

Edge Scheduling

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.OpticalTerminal — Type
OpticalTerminal(; boresight, field_of_regard_rad, frame=:rtn)

One steerable optical terminal of a spacecraft in an EdgeFeasibilityModel. It can point anywhere within field_of_regard_rad (in (0, π]) of boresight, a direction in frame:

  • :rtn: the spacecraft's own radial, along-track and cross-track axes, from its position and velocity, for a spacecraft that keeps a local-orbital attitude. The convention is that of the RPO frame utilities: radial along the position, cross-track along the orbit normal, along-track completing the set.
  • :body: its body frame, through the attitude of its state sample, which must have one. terminal_pose(spacecraft, terminal) gives a mounted terminal's boresight in that frame.
  • :inertial: the inertial frame.

A direction exactly field_of_regard_rad from the boresight is inside, as for TerminalPointing. A terminal carries at most one active link at a time.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.MinimumForceRange — Type
MinimumForceRange(; min_force_n)

A range against power for an EdgeFeasibilityModel: the largest separation ρ_max(P) at which its laser link, transmitting P watts, still exerts at least min_force_n newtons on each end (power_limited_range_m).

  • With a GaussianBeam. The force is magnification × P / c times the beam's capture fraction at the far aperture (beam_capture_fraction), which falls with range. With η = F_min c / (magnification × P), the waist radius w0, the aperture radius a and the Rayleigh range z_R = π w0² / λ, ρ_max = z_R √(w² / w0² − 1) for the spot radius w² = 2a² / (−ln(1 − η)) at which the capture fraction is η.
  • Limits. ρ_max is zero when even the waist captures less than η (or η ≥ 1), and unbounded when η ≤ 0.
  • Without a beam the force does not fall with range: ρ_max is unbounded when magnification × P / c ≥ F_min and zero otherwise.

The link's own range_m caps ρ_max in the feasible-edge graph. min_force_n must be finite and nonnegative.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.power_limited_range_m — Function
power_limited_range_m(limit, link, power_w) -> Float64

The range ρ_max(P) (m) up to which link can close while transmitting power_w watts, by the range-against-power rule limit of an EdgeFeasibilityModel: a MinimumForceRange, any function of the power in watts returning metres, a positive number of metres (a cap that does not depend on the power), or nothing for no power dependence (Inf). The feasible-edge graph uses min(link_range_m(link), ρ_max(P)).

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.DutyCycleLimit — Type
DutyCycleLimit(; on_steps, window_steps, scope=:spacecraft)

A duty-cycle limit on laser links over a grid of planning steps: in any window_steps consecutive steps, at most on_steps active link-steps

  • at each spacecraft (scope=:spacecraft): each active link of the spacecraft counts once in each step it is active, so two links in one step count two;
  • on each link (scope=:edge): the steps in which that link is active.

on_steps must be a nonnegative integer and window_steps a positive one.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.EdgeFeasibilityModel — Type
EdgeFeasibilityModel(link, terminals; pairs=all_pairs(terminals), power_w=nothing,
                     max_range=nothing, duty_cycle=nothing)

The rule that decides which spacecraft pairs of a constellation could carry a laser link at a sample time (feasible_edge_graph). An edge (i, j) is feasible when

  1. line of sight: with the link's occultation, the segment between the two spacecraft clears the planet's sphere raised by its min_ray_altitude_m, as for the link in a run;
  2. range against power: 0 < ρ_ij ≤ min(link_range_m(link), ρ_max(P_ij)), with ρ_max from max_range (power_limited_range_m) and P_ij the pair's transmitted power;
  3. field of regard at both ends: some terminal of i sees ℓ̂_ij, the unit line of sight from i to j, within its field of regard, and some terminal of j sees −ℓ̂_ij;
  4. terminal and duty-cycle limits: an edge needs a terminal at each end (3); a set of edges active together needs distinct terminals (terminal_assignment); and with duty_cycle, the edge must stay within the DutyCycleLimit given the steps before (previous).
  • link is a LaserBeamLink (a LaserMomentumLink is taken as the beam link with its defaults) without terminal pointing or mounted recoil: the field of regard comes from terminals, and the geometry is the spacecraft body-reference positions.
  • terminals holds, for each spacecraft by vector index, its OpticalTerminals.
  • pairs lists the candidate pairs (i, j); each is kept as (min, max).
  • power_w is the transmitted power (W) of every pair, a vector with one entry per pair, or a function (i, j) -> P; nothing takes the link's power_w.
  • max_range is the range-against-power rule (power_limited_range_m).
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.FeasibleEdgeGraph — Type
FeasibleEdgeGraph

The feasible-edge graph G_k at one sample time, from feasible_edge_graph. Each field is a vector over pairs, in the model's order, unless noted.

  • time_s: the sample time (s), from the LinkContext.
  • pairs: the candidate pairs (i, j), i < j.
  • feasible: whether each edge is feasible: all of the flags below.
  • line_of_sight, in_range, duty_cycle: each constraint's flag.
  • field_of_regard: (at i, at j): whether some terminal at that end sees the other end within its field of regard.
  • range_m, max_range_m: the separation and its limit min(link_range_m, ρ_max(P)).
  • direction: ℓ̂_ij, the inertial unit line of sight from i to j.
  • terminal_options: (at i, at j): the numbers of the terminals at each end that see the other end.
  • terminal_count: each spacecraft's number of terminals (by vector index).
  • duty_limit: the model's DutyCycleLimit, or nothing.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.feasible_edge_graph — Function
feasible_edge_graph(model::EdgeFeasibilityModel, samples; context=LinkContext(), previous=()) -> FeasibleEdgeGraph
feasible_edge_graph(model::EdgeFeasibilityModel, state; spacecraft=nothing, context=LinkContext(), previous=())

The feasible-edge graph G_k of model at one sample time.

  • samples holds one StateSample per spacecraft, by vector index; state is a run state, whose spacecraft i is state.sc[i] (as a solution sol(t) of run_simulation), with the run's spacecraft models as spacecraft when known. A :body terminal needs the sample's attitude.
  • context gives the time and the planet (any value with an equatorial radius Rp_e); without a planet, nothing blocks the line of sight.
  • previous lists the active edge sets of the steps just before this one, oldest first, each a collection of pairs. With a DutyCycleLimit, an edge is feasible only if activating it at this step, alone, keeps every window that ends here within the limit. Edges active together at this step share the budget: admissible_edge_set checks a whole set.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.feasible_edge_graphs — Function
feasible_edge_graphs(model::EdgeFeasibilityModel, states, times; planet=nothing, spacecraft=nothing) -> Vector{FeasibleEdgeGraph}

The feasible-edge graphs G_k of a propagated constellation at the sample times times (s): states(t) returns the constellation's run state at time t, as the solution of run_simulation(...; return_solution=true) does. planet is the run's planet (for the line of sight), and spacecraft its spacecraft models when known. No activity precedes the samples, so each graph's duty-cycle flags say only whether an edge alone fits the limit; the scheduler applies the limit to its whole schedule.

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.feasible_pairs — Function
feasible_pairs(graph::FeasibleEdgeGraph) -> Vector{Tuple{Int, Int}}

The pairs of graph that are feasible, in the graph's order.

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.terminal_assignment — Function
terminal_assignment(graph::FeasibleEdgeGraph, active) -> Union{Nothing, Dict{Tuple{Int, Int}, NTuple{2, Int}}}

A terminal for each end of every pair in active such that no terminal carries two links, each terminal seeing the other end within its field of regard (graph.terminal_options): a dictionary from each pair to its terminal numbers (at i, at j). nothing when no such assignment exists, as when a spacecraft has fewer terminals able to point than active links. Each spacecraft's terminals are matched to its active links by augmenting paths, so an assignment is found whenever one exists.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.InterLinkModels.admissible_edge_set — Function
admissible_edge_set(graph::FeasibleEdgeGraph, active; max_active=nothing, previous=()) -> Bool

Whether the edges active (a collection of pairs) can be active together at the graph's step: each is a candidate whose line of sight, range and field of regard hold, there are at most max_active of them, a terminal_assignment exists, and with the graph's DutyCycleLimit, the step's edges, after the steps previous (oldest first), keep every window that ends here within it.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.EdgeLinearStep — Type
EdgeLinearStep(A, B, edges; columns=nothing)
EdgeLinearStep(A, blocks::AbstractVector, edges)

One step k of a linear time-varying model whose inputs are link forces, δx_{k+1} = A_k δx_k + Σ_e B_k^e δu_{k,e}, in any state representation.

  • A is the n × n state transition of the step.
  • edges lists the candidate links (i, j) (spacecraft vector indices; kept as (min, max)), and B (n × m) holds their input columns: the block B[:, columns[e]] belongs to edges[e]. By default each edge has one column, its force magnitude, in order.
  • The second form takes one block per edge (a vector for one column, or an n × m_e matrix) and builds B and columns from them.

The inputs are in the units the columns are scaled to: one newton of link force for edge_input_matrix. A step from another representation (orbital elements, Kustaanheimo–Stiefel coordinates) needs only its own A_k and the input columns of each candidate edge.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.EdgeLinearModel — Type
EdgeLinearModel(steps, projection=I)
EdgeLinearModel(As, Bs, edges, projection=I)

A window of N steps k₀ … k₀+N−1 of a linear time-varying model with link inputs (EdgeLinearStep), and the map projection (r × n, r ≥ 1) from the state at the window's end, δx_{k₀+N}, to the coordinates whose controllability is assessed. This is the scheduler's representation-agnostic interface: a sequence of state transitions A_k with each candidate edge's input columns B_k^e, and the output map C and the barycentric transformation T_rel folded into projection.

  • The relative subspace. The link forces have no barycenter component at any instant (the continuous B_c), so for a stacked Cartesian state projection = T[relative_rows, :] from barycentric_transform drops a mode they cannot drive directly. Over a window in orbit, unequal gravity couples the barycenter weakly to the relative motion, so a discretized B_k has small, nonzero barycenter rows.
  • Rigid rotation. The relative rows still hold the formation's rigid rotation, which line-of-centers forces cannot drive in free space and drive only weakly in orbit (they leave the total angular momentum about the planet's center unchanged). For λ_min and log det, prefer a rotation-free output such as range_output_matrix.
  • Units. Rows in different units (metres and metres per second) spread the Gramian's eigenvalues by the units alone; scale them to one unit, for example the velocity or rate rows by 1 / n for the mean motion n.
  • Physical outputs. For an output δy = C δz_rel (ranges, an RTN formation state), projection = C * T[relative_rows, :]. A model in other coordinates first maps to Cartesian ones with its own output matrix at the window's end: projection = T[relative_rows, :] * C_cart.
  • Requirements. Every step lists the same edges in the same order and has the state dimension of projection's columns. As, Bs give one A_k and one B_k (one column per edge) per step. In a model whose spacecraft keep their own time variables (Kustaanheimo–Stiefel fictitious time), each step must span one common physical-time interval for every spacecraft, so that both ends of a link are forced together.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.barycentric_transform — Function
barycentric_transform(masses; block_size=6, relative=:chief) -> (; T, barycenter_rows, relative_rows)

The transformation T_rel from a stacked state of length(masses) spacecraft blocks of block_size entries each to barycenter and relative coordinates, z = T x. Every entry of a block must add with mass, as Cartesian position and velocity components do.

  • Rows barycenter_rows (the first block) are the barycenter, Σ m_i x_i / Σ m_i.
  • Rows relative_rows are the relative coordinates of spacecraft 2 … N: x_i − x_1 with relative=:chief, or x_i − x_cm with relative=:barycenter.

T is invertible. Masses must be finite and positive.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.edge_input_matrix — Function
edge_input_matrix(graph::FeasibleEdgeGraph, masses; input_maps=:cartesian, edges=graph.pairs) -> Matrix{Float64}

The continuous input matrix B_c (n × length(edges)) of a constellation's link forces at the geometry of graph: column e is the state rate per newton of the link edges[e] = (i, j).

  • Sign. A positive input pushes the two ends apart along the line of sight, as the laser links' link_force does: spacecraft i accelerates by −ℓ̂_ij / m_i and j by +ℓ̂_ij / m_j, with ℓ̂_ij the graph's direction. A negative input pulls them together, which a laser cannot do: a model linearized about zero link force can use only nonnegative inputs. Giving an edge as (j, i) does not change its column.
  • Input maps. input_maps maps each spacecraft's inertial acceleration into the state rate: a vector with one n × 3 matrix per spacecraft, or :cartesian for stacked [r_i; v_i] blocks of six.
  • The sign does not change a Gramian (each column enters as g gᵀ), but it does change the inputs of minimum_input_energy.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.zoh_discretize — Function
zoh_discretize(Ac, Bc, dt) -> (A, B)

The zero-order-hold discretization of ẋ = Ac x + Bc u over a step of dt: A = exp(Ac dt), B = ∫₀^dt exp(Ac τ) dτ Bc, both from the exponential of the augmented matrix [Ac Bc; 0 0] dt.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.link_force_jacobian — Function
link_force_jacobian(graph::FeasibleEdgeGraph, masses, forces; edges=graph.pairs) -> Matrix{Float64}

The state Jacobian, for stacked Cartesian states [r_i; v_i], of nominal link forces forces[e] ≥ 0 (N) held on edges at the geometry of graph. A model linearized about a positive nominal force adds it to its continuous Ac; the signed inputs about that force are then perturbations within −F* ≤ δu ≤ F_max − F*.

Each force pushes its ends apart along ℓ̂_ij, which turns with the relative position: the force on j changes by (F*/ρ)(I − ℓ̂ℓ̂ᵀ)(δr_j − δr_i) and the force on i by its negative. The force's magnitude is held fixed; a beam force that falls with range adds a radial term that is not included.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.range_output_matrix — Function
range_output_matrix(samples, pairs; rates=true, rate_scale=1.0) -> Matrix{Float64}
range_output_matrix(state, pairs; rates=true, rate_scale=1.0)

An output map for stacked Cartesian states [r_i; v_i]: the first-order change of the range ρ_ij of each pair in pairs, then, with rates, of each range rate ρ̇_ij times rate_scale, at the given states (one StateSample per spacecraft, or a run state with spacecraft i at state.sc[i]).

These outputs ignore the formation's rigid motion: a translation, or a rotation of every position and velocity together, changes none of them. Used as the projection of an EdgeLinearModel, they keep λ_min and log det on the formation's shape. rate_scale = 1 / n, for the mean motion n, puts the rates in metres like the ranges.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.reachability_matrix — Function
reachability_matrix(model::EdgeLinearModel; active=nothing) -> (; matrix, columns)

The projected reachability matrix of the window, R_N(k₀) = P [Φ(k₀+N, k₀+1) B_{k₀} … B_{k₀+N−1}] with Φ(k, j) = A_{k−1} ⋯ A_j, restricted to the active links: active[k] lists the edges active at step k (all edges at every step when nothing). columns[c] names column c as (step, edge, input), the input being the column's place in the edge's block.

With the projection P a physical output map, column (k, e) is the change of that output at the window's end per unit input on edge e at step k: the terminal gain of that activation.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.controllability_gramian — Function
controllability_gramian(model::EdgeLinearModel; active=nothing) -> Matrix{Float64}

The finite-horizon controllability Gramian of the window, W_N(k₀) = R_N R_Nᵀ (reachability_matrix), in the projected coordinates: with the relative projection, the relative-subspace Gramian; with an output map, the output Gramian W_N^y. active as for reachability_matrix.

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.AbstractControllabilityMetric — Type
AbstractControllabilityMetric

Supertype of the scalar measures Ψ(W) of a controllability Gramian that the edge schedules maximize: LargestEigenvalueMetric, SmallestEigenvalueMetric, LogDetMetric and DirectionEnergyMetric. A metric implements controllability_metric. Each built-in metric is nondecreasing as links are added, since every activation adds a positive semidefinite term to W. The schedules count two values as equal within the roundoff of a symmetric eigensolver (eps · n · λ_max, with the trace for λ_max), scaled to the metric; a metric of one's own gets that eigenvalue tolerance.

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.LargestEigenvalueMetric — Type
LargestEigenvalueMetric()

λ₁(W): the input energy reach of the easiest direction, whose eigenvector is the dominant deformation mode.

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.SmallestEigenvalueMetric — Type
SmallestEigenvalueMetric()

λ_min(W): the reach of the hardest direction. It stays zero until the active links make every projected direction reachable, so it needs a projection on which that can happen: one in common units, without the barycenter or the rigid rotation (range_output_matrix).

source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.LogDetMetric — Type
LogDetMetric(; epsilon)

log det(W + ε I): the log volume of the reachable ellipsoid, with the regularizer ε > 0 (finite, in the units of W). Directions whose eigenvalue lies below ε count little, so choose ε against the spectrum of a projection in common units.

DirectionEnergyMetric(direction)

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.gramian_metrics — Function
gramian_metrics(W; epsilon, direction=nothing) -> NamedTuple

Every metric of the Gramian W at once, with its eigenstructure: largest_eigenvalue (λ₁), smallest_eigenvalue (λ_min), log_det (log det(W + εI) with epsilon), direction_energy (dᵀWd, NaN without a direction), eigenvalues (ascending), modes (the matching unit eigenvectors, by column) and dominant_mode (v₁, the eigenvector of λ₁).

AbstractEdgeScheduleStrategy

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.ConstantEdgeSet — Type
ConstantEdgeSet(; edges=nothing, metric=nothing, tie_break=nothing)

One link set kept over the whole window: at each step, its edges that are admissible there (feasible, a free terminal at each end, within the duty cycle), in the set's order.

  • With edges, that set (at most max_active edges).
  • Otherwise the set is built from the candidate edges by metric: each round adds the edge whose constant use gives the largest metric, until max_active edges. Ties (within the roundoff tolerance of GreedyEdgeSchedule) go to tie_break, a second metric, and then to the earlier edge.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.GreedyEdgeSchedule — Type
GreedyEdgeSchedule(metric; tie_break=nothing)

A schedule built one activation at a time over the whole window: each round adds the (step, edge) activation, among those still admissible, that gives the largest metric of the Gramian.

  • Ties. Two values within the roundoff of a symmetric eigensolver (about eps · n · λ_max, carried into the metric) are equal. A tie goes to tie_break, a second metric compared the same way, and then to the earlier step and edge. EdgeSchedule.tied_rounds counts the rounds in which every candidate tied on metric, so that the tie break or the order chose.
  • Filling. It fills every admissible slot: the built-in metrics never fall as links are added, so the metric decides which links are active, not how many. When no limit binds, every strategy gives the same all-feasible schedule; strategies differ only under a binding max_active, terminal or duty-cycle limit.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(LargestEigenvalueMetric()) is the greedy λ₁ schedule.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(LogDetMetric(; epsilon)) is the greedy volume schedule. log det(W + εI) is monotone and submodular in the activations, the property greedy approximation bounds rest on; no bound is claimed here under terminal and duty-cycle limits.
  • GreedyEdgeSchedule(SmallestEigenvalueMetric(); tie_break=LogDetMetric(; epsilon)) is the greedy λ_min schedule. λ_min stays zero until every projected direction is reachable, so the tie break chooses until then; on a projection that keeps directions the links cannot reach, it chooses throughout.
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.EdgeSchedule — Type
EdgeSchedule

The active links of a window, from schedule_edges.

  • active[k]: the edges active at step k, in the model's edge order (the active argument of reachability_matrix and controllability_gramian).
  • gramian: the window's projected Gramian under this schedule.
  • terminals[k]: with feasible-edge graphs, each active edge's terminals (at i, at j) at step k (terminal_assignment); empty otherwise.
  • tied_rounds: the selection rounds in which every admissible candidate tied on the strategy's metric, so that its tie break or the candidate order chose (0 for a given constant set).
source
SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.schedule_edges — Function
schedule_edges(model::EdgeLinearModel, strategy; max_active, graphs=nothing, feasible=nothing,
               duty_cycle=nothing, previous=()) -> EdgeSchedule

The active links σ_k of each step of the window model, chosen by strategy (ConstantEdgeSet, GreedyEdgeSchedule) to make the Gramian large, subject to σ_k ⊆ E_k and |σ_k| ≤ max_active.

  • graphs gives the feasible-edge graph G_k of each step (feasible_edge_graph): its line of sight, range and field-of-regard flags give E_k, and a step's set must have a terminal_assignment. Each model edge must be a pair of every graph.
  • Or feasible, an edges × steps Boolean matrix, gives E_k alone, with no terminal limit; with neither, every edge is feasible at every step.
  • duty_cycle limits the schedule over the window, counting the steps previous (active sets, oldest first) just before it. By default it is the graphs' DutyCycleLimit, which must then be the same in every graph.

SpaceAGORA.SimulationModel.ControllabilityScheduling.minimum_input_energy — Function
minimum_input_energy(model::EdgeLinearModel, schedule, target; initial_state=nothing, rtol=1e-10, reach_tol=1e-8)
    -> (; energy, inputs, residual, reachable, most_negative_input, nonnegative)

The least input energy Σ |δu|² that moves the window's projected end state to target with the links of schedule (an EdgeSchedule, or active sets as for reachability_matrix), from δx_{k₀} = 0 or from initial_state, whose free response the inputs then correct.

  • Energy and inputs. energy is yᵀ W⁺ y, the minimum-norm solution of R u = y for the required change y, with singular values below rtol times the largest dropped. inputs[k] maps each edge active at step k to its input vector.
  • Reach. residual is |R u − y| / |y| (zero for y = 0), and reachable whether it is at most reach_tol: a target outside the reachable subspace gets the least-squares inputs, reachable = false.
  • Signs. The inputs are signed, in the sign of the model's columns: for edge_input_matrix, a positive input pushes the ends apart and a negative one pulls them together. most_negative_input is the smallest input (0 when none is negative), and nonnegative whether every input is at least −√eps times the largest magnitude. About zero link force, a laser that only pushes realizes the solution only when nonnegative holds; otherwise the energy is that of a two-way actuator, a lower bound on the push-only one.
