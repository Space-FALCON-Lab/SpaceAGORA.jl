Interlinks

Use this page to connect spacecraft through interlinks, with a policy that selects which links are active.

What to read next:

  • Configuring a Simulation
  • Parallel Execution

Interlinks and constellations

An interlink is a connection between two spacecraft terminals that does something while the geometry allows it: push the spacecraft apart, carry power, or carry data. A constellation is a set of spacecraft and their orbits, laid out and run together. They are different things: a constellation needs no interlinks, and two spacecraft can be linked without forming one.

Links and link kinds

Each candidate link has its own kind, a value of a subtype of AbstractInterlink. The kind decides when the link can close and what it does to its two endpoints:

  • link_range_m(link): the maximum separation in metres. Every kind implements it.
  • link_available(link, a, b, ctx): whether the link can close. By default, the endpoints are apart and within link_range_m.
  • link_force(link, a, b, ctx): the inertial forces (N) on a and b while the link is active. By default, none.

a and b are the endpoints' state samples (StateSample: position, velocity, mass, attitude and spacecraft_index), in the order of the link's key: the lower (spacecraft, terminal) pair first, or, for a directed link, the transmitter first. ctx is a LinkContext holding the time (s into the run) and the run's planet.

The built-in kinds are:

  • LaserMomentumLink(; power_w, magnification, range_m): an open-cavity laser link with the idealized central force magnification * power_w / c, directed away from the other endpoint, equal and opposite on the two spacecraft. LaserMomentumLink(terminal) takes the numbers of a LaserTerminal.
  • LaserBeamLink(; power_w, magnification, range_m, ...): the same link, with opt-in occultation, beam divergence, pointing and mounted recoil. See Laser physics.
  • PowerBeamLink(; power_w, wavelength_m, waist_radius_m, range_m): a power beam from a LaserTerminal to a PowerReceiver. See Power links.
  • DataLink(; transmit_power_w, budget, range_m): a one-way data link, from a RadioTerminal or a virtual terminal to another, at the rate its link budget sets. See Data links.

Setting up interlinks

An InterlinkModel holds the candidate links, spacecraft pairs that may never link, and the history of the links the scheduler selected. Endpoints are (spacecraft vector index, terminal number) pairs: the spacecraft's position in dynamics_model.spacecraft, not its id. Each terminal can take part in at most one selected link.

model = InterlinkModel()
for partner in 2:3
    register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, 1), (partner, 1),
        LaserMomentumLink(power_w=10_000.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3))
end
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model,
    scheduling_policy_model=SemimajorAxisRatePolicy())

Start with examples/gve_sma_interlinks.jl. It builds a three-spacecraft case, registers two candidate links, and selects the link that increases the target's instantaneous semimajor-axis rate. examples/targeted_40sat_sma_interlinks.jl uses the same interface with thirty helpers and ten targets. These are analytical atmosphere examples; they do not require native GRAM or private telemetry.

include("examples/gve_sma_interlinks.jl")
args = gve_sma_configuration(duration=120.0)
solution = run_simulation(args; return_solution=true)
history = solution.prob.p.args.interlink_model.history

Terminals

A spacecraft's terminals are its n_terminal virtual terminals, numbered 1:n_terminal, followed by the terminal hardware mounted on it, in mounting order. A LaserTerminal mounted with add_actuator! is terminal hardware; a kind of your own opts in with SpaceAGORA.link_terminal(::MyTerminal) = true. terminal_count(spacecraft) gives the total, and mounted_link_terminals(spacecraft) lists the hardware with its numbers. A spacecraft with only hardware sets n_terminal=0. Virtual terminals stay for idealized studies, which run as before.

An endpoint's terminal is a number, or the mounted terminal itself:

bus = Link(root=true, m=100.0)
sc = SpacecraftModel(root=bus, links=[bus], inertia_tensor=bus.inertia, n_terminal=0)
terminal = LaserTerminal(boresight=[0.0, 1.0, 0.0], max_power_w=50.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3)
add_actuator!(sc, bus, terminal)
# ... a second spacecraft, `other`, likewise
spacecraft = [sc, other]
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, terminal), (2, 1), LaserMomentumLink(terminal))

A terminal must be able to carry its link (terminal_supports(terminal, link)). A LaserTerminal carries LaserMomentumLink, and a virtual terminal carries any kind, except a DataLink whose ShannonBudget reads the radios at both ends. terminal_number(spacecraft, terminal) gives a mounted terminal's number. Two mounted terminals with equal fields cannot be told apart, so for those give the number.

Directed links

A kind with link_directed(link) = true runs one way: from the first endpoint given to register_link! (the transmitter) to the second (the receiver). Its key keeps that order, so its hooks receive the transmitter as a. The opposite direction between the same terminals is a separate link. Laser momentum links are undirected.

Registering many links

register_links!(model, spacecraft, pairs, link) registers one link per spacecraft pair. all_pairs(spacecraft) gives every pair, or you can pass any vector of (i, j) index pairs. For a constellation laid out by build_constellation or walker_constellation (see Constellations), its planes and slots give the usual topologies:

  • same_plane_pairs(spacecraft): every pair within each plane.
  • ring_pairs(spacecraft): each slot with the next in its plane, closing the ring.
  • grid_pairs(spacecraft; wrap_planes=true): the "+grid", the rings plus each slot with the same slot of the next plane. Pass wrap_planes=false for a Walker star, whose first and last planes counter-rotate. link is a kind value, or a function

(i, j) -> link. Each spacecraft's links take its terminals that can carry them in turn, starting again from the first when they run out; links that share a terminal compete for it when the scheduler selects. Pass terminals=(i, j) -> (terminal_i, terminal_j) to choose the terminals yourself.

register_links!(model, spacecraft, all_pairs(spacecraft), LaserMomentumLink(power_w=40.0))
# or, for a constellation's +grid:
register_links!(model, spacecraft, grid_pairs(spacecraft), LaserMomentumLink(power_w=40.0))

Terminal hardware and power

A hardware terminal that is a link endpoint is driven by its links for the whole run:

  • Its level. While one of its links is active, it runs at the level the link sets, link_terminal_levels(link). For a LaserMomentumLink, that is the link's power_w at both ends. Otherwise its level is 0.
  • Its power. It draws actuator_consumption(terminal, level) from its spacecraft's power budget: power_w / efficiency for a LaserTerminal. The draw shows in the power columns, and in the actuator_level and actuator_power columns.
  • Its force. Only the link's force applies while the link is active. The terminal's own recoil applies only when a controller drives a terminal that is no link's endpoint.
  • Controllers. A mounted-actuation controller's levels for a link-driven terminal apply nothing, and setup says so once.
  • Limits. A link that would drive its terminal past its limits (above a LaserTerminal's max_power_w) is refused at setup. Build the link from the terminal, LaserMomentumLink(terminal), to match them.

When the battery sheds tier 1 (see Powering a Spacecraft), it sheds laser and radio terminals. Links through shed hardware terminals then become unavailable, and stay so until the tier clears: a shed terminal keeps asking for its link's power, so the tier clears when the battery can supply it again. A terminal kind's tier is terminal_shed_tier(terminal): 1 for LaserTerminal and RadioTerminal, 0 (never shed) for PowerReceiver and by default. Virtual terminals draw nothing and are never shed.

Laser physics

LaserBeamLink is the open-cavity laser link with four effects you can switch on. With none of them it is LaserMomentumLink, bit for bit.

beam = GaussianBeam(wavelength_m=1.064e-6, waist_radius_m=0.05, aperture_radius_m=0.08)
link = LaserBeamLink(power_w=10_000.0, magnification=100.0, range_m=200e3;
    occultation=(; min_ray_altitude_m=100e3),   # or true
    beam,
    pointing=TerminalPointing(field_of_regard_rad=deg2rad(30)),
    recoil=:mounted)
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, terminal_1), (2, terminal_2), link)
  • Line of sight (occultation=true). The link cannot close while the segment between the two spacecraft meets the planet, a sphere of its equatorial radius. occultation=(; min_ray_altitude_m=h) raises that sphere by h metres, so that a beam grazing the atmosphere counts as blocked. The atmosphere's attenuation is not modelled.
  • Divergence (beam). The force is scaled by beam_force_factor(beam, link, terminal_a, terminal_b, d) at separation d.
    • For a GaussianBeam, this is the share of the beam that the far aperture captures, 1 − exp(−2 r² / w(d)²), from the same beam functions as a power link (beam_spot_radius_m, beam_capture_fraction).
    • Within the Rayleigh range π w0² / λ, the force is close to the near-field force magnification × power_w / c. Far beyond it, the force falls as 1/d².
    • This is an engineering model, not a cavity model: it takes the magnification as the near-field value and scales it by one pass's geometric capture.
    • A beam model of your own subtypes AbstractLaserBeam and implements beam_force_factor, for example a recycling cavity or a measured curve.
  • Pointing (pointing=TerminalPointing(; field_of_regard_rad, error_rad=0.0)).
    • The link can close only while each end's line of sight to the other lies within field_of_regard_rad of its terminal's boresight, read in the spacecraft's attitude. Within that cone the terminal points ideally: there is no slew rate or gimbal state.
    • A static pointing error error_rad scales the force by exp(−2 (error / θ)²), with θ = λ / (π w0) the beam's divergence. It needs a GaussianBeam. Jitter is not modelled.
  • Mounted recoil (recoil=:mounted). Each end's force acts at its terminal's mounted location (terminal_pose), so it turns the spacecraft with the torque of the terminal's lever arm, as the terminal's own recoil would. The default, :central, applies the force with no torque.

The solver does not locate occultation or field-of-regard boundary crossings as events. Link availability and selection are recomputed at each accepted step; a crossing between steps is not resolved at its exact time. Check timestep convergence when these boundaries matter for your case.

Pointing and mounted recoil need terminal hardware at both ends (a LaserTerminal carries a LaserBeamLink), and attitude (orientation_sim=true). register_link! refuses a virtual terminal for them, and a run without attitude refuses them at setup. With either option, range, occultation, divergence and force direction all use the ray between the mounted terminals, including the attitude transformation of each mount offset. Other configurations retain the spacecraft body-reference positions. Call the terminal-aware interlink_wrench and link_available forms for pointing or mounted recoil; their four-argument force/availability forms lack the required terminal hardware.

The two forces are always equal and opposite, so the link conserves the run's linear momentum. While the link is active, the terminals' own recoil applies nothing: the link's force is their recoil (see Terminal hardware and power). A checkpoint records every option, and a resume with any of them changed is refused. The gve example runs with a beam when given one: gve_sma_configuration(beam=GaussianBeam(...)).

Power links

A PowerBeamLink carries power, one way, from a LaserTerminal (the transmitter) to a PowerReceiver (the receiver). It applies no force. This configuration fragment uses the 50 W terminal mounted above and requests 40 W, within its limit:

receiver = PowerReceiver(normal=[0.0, -1.0, 0.0], radius_m=0.25, efficiency=0.4)
add_actuator!(other, other.root, receiver)
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, terminal), (2, receiver),
    PowerBeamLink(power_w=40.0, wavelength_m=1.064e-6, waist_radius_m=0.05, range_m=50e3))
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model,
    scheduling_policy_model=PowerDeliveryPolicy())
  • The beam. While the link is active, the transmitter runs at power_w optical watts. It draws power_w / efficiency from its own battery as a tier-1 laser load (see Terminal hardware and power). The beam is Gaussian:
    • its waist, of 1/e² radius waist_radius_m, is at the transmitter;
    • its spot radius at range d, beam_spot_radius_m(link, d), is w0 √(1 + (d / z_R)²), with the Rayleigh range z_R = π w0² / λ.
  • The receiver. It captures beam_capture_fraction(link, radius_m, d), 1 − exp(−2 r² / w²), of the beam through its aperture. It delivers that times its efficiency to its bus, up to its max_power_w (link_received_power_w).
    • For example, with a 1.064 µm beam from a 5 cm waist, a 25 cm receiver catches nearly all of it out to about 10 km, about a quarter at 100 km, and under 1 % at 1000 km.
  • Generation. The delivered power is generation in the receiver's power budget. It shows in power_generated_w and in its own power_received_w column, charges the receiver's battery, and helps an empty battery recover. A full receiver battery curtails it, as it does solar power; the transmitter keeps paying.
  • Availability. The link can close within range_m, while the segment between the spacecraft clears the planet (a sphere of its equatorial radius). When the transmitter's battery sheds tier 1, the beam becomes unavailable until the tier clears.
  • Direction. A PowerReceiver can only receive, and a LaserTerminal can only transmit a power beam (terminal_supports(terminal, link, side)).
  • Scheduling. Policies that score links by their forces never select a power beam, so use PowerDeliveryPolicy(), which scores each link by the power it would deliver, or EnergyBalancingPolicy(), which charges the lowest state of charge (see Energy balancing). To weigh momentum and power links together, use a weighted mix.
  • Columns. A run with a power beam adds power_received_w and power_beamed_w per spacecraft to the power columns.
  • Not yet modelled. Pointing and jitter, the receiver's incidence angle, the atmosphere, beam quality, and the beam's radiation pressure are not modelled.

Data links

A DataLink carries data, one way, from a transmitter terminal to a receiver. It applies no force. Its budget sets the rate:

radio() = RadioTerminal(boresight=[0.0, 1.0, 0.0], max_power_w=5.0, gain_dbi=6.0, efficiency=0.5,
    noise_temperature_k=500.0)
sender, receiver = radio(), radio()
add_actuator!(sc, bus, sender)
add_actuator!(other, other.root, receiver)
link = DataLink(transmit_power_w=1.0, range_m=2000e3,
    budget=ShannonBudget(bandwidth_hz=1e6, frequency_hz=2.2e9, implementation_efficiency=0.5))
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, sender), (2, receiver), link)
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model,
    scheduling_policy_model=DataThroughputPolicy())
  • The budget.
    • FixedRateBudget(; rate_bps): a constant rate at any range up to range_m. It works between virtual terminals too.
    • ShannonBudget(; bandwidth_hz, frequency_hz, implementation_efficiency=0.5, max_rate_bps=Inf): a radio link budget between two RadioTerminals.
      • The received power follows Friis: P_r = P_t G_t G_r (λ / 4πd)², with the link's transmit_power_w as P_t, the two radios' gain_dbi and λ = c / frequency_hz.
      • The noise is k_B T bandwidth_hz, with the receiver's noise_temperature_k as T.
      • The rate is implementation_efficiency × bandwidth_hz × log₂(1 + P_r / N), up to max_rate_bps.
      • For example, in the code above the rate is about 5.7 Mbit/s at 10 km, 2.4 Mbit/s at 100 km and 0.17 Mbit/s at 1000 km.
    • A budget kind of your own subtypes AbstractDataBudget and implements budget_rate_bps(budget, link, transmitter, receiver, distance_m). The terminals are nothing at a virtual end.
    • The rate is capped at each RadioTerminal's max_rate_bps (link_data_rate_bps).
  • The radios. A RadioTerminal can transmit or receive. While it transmits, it runs at the link's transmit_power_w and draws transmit_power_w / efficiency, as a tier-1 load. A receiving radio draws nothing; give its electronics a constant PowerLoad. When the transmitter's battery sheds tier 1, the link becomes unavailable until the tier clears. A LaserTerminal or a PowerReceiver cannot carry a data link.
  • Bits. In a run with a data link, every spacecraft has two more integrated states, data_sent_bits and data_received_bits. Their rates are the sums of the rates its active links carry out and in, evaluated at every right-hand-side call. The solver integrates these counters alongside the other states, subject to its integration tolerances. They continue across a checkpoint resume.
  • Availability. As for a power beam: within range_m, while the segment between the spacecraft clears the planet.
  • Direction. Two-way traffic is two links, one each way. A radio carries one active link at a time, so the scheduler selects one direction of a two-way pair between the same two radios: the one with the higher score.
  • Scheduling. Use DataThroughputPolicy(), which scores each link by the rate it would carry. The force and power policies never select a data link.
  • Columns. A run with a data link adds data_rate_tx_bps, data_rate_rx_bps, data_sent_bits and data_received_bits per spacecraft (see the data columns).
  • Not yet modelled. Pointing and antenna patterns, Doppler, atmospheric and polarization losses, interference between links and acquisition time are not modelled. Onboard storage and ground stations are below.

Data storage

A spacecraft can hold data in a DataStore and produce it with data sources. The data links then carry only what the stores hold, or what passes through them:

add_data!(sc, bus, DataStore(capacity_bits=8e9, initial_bits=0.0))   # the root link, at most one
add_data!(sc, bus, DataSource(rate_bps=2e5))                          # any link, any number

  • States. In a run with a store, every spacecraft has three more integrated states, which stay zero for a spacecraft without a store:

    • data_stored_bits, the data its store holds;
    • data_produced_bits, what its sources have produced;
    • data_lost_bits, the production a full store could not keep.

    They balance: stored = initial + produced + received − sent − lost. The counters data_sent_bits and data_received_bits count what the links actually carry.

  • Flow control. A link carries at most its rate (link_data_rate_bps), and less at a store, so links lose nothing:

    • An empty store passes what comes in straight on: its production and what its incoming links carry, shared among its outgoing links in proportion to their rates. A relay with an empty store forwards data at zero stock.
    • A full store takes in no more than it sends on. Its production beyond that is lost and counted in data_lost_bits.
    • A spacecraft without a store neither limits its links nor is limited, as before.

    The rates are found at every right-hand-side evaluation, along chains of relays.

  • Full and empty. A store counts as full within 1e-9 of its capacity and as empty within 1e-9 of its capacity of nothing. Events mark it full or empty when it crosses either, as a battery's do, and the flags are checked again at the end of each step. They continue across a checkpoint resume. A resume with another capacity or initial content is refused.

  • Sources. DataSource(; rate_bps, name="") produces a constant rate while its spacecraft is active. A source kind of your own subtypes AbstractDataSource and implements data_production_bps(source, x::StateSample, ctx::LinkContext, t), its rate in bit/s: for example imaging only over a region, a duty cycle, or a rate that depends on the attitude. A run refuses at setup a source on a spacecraft without a store, and a source kind without a method.

  • Solvers. As with a battery, a run with a data store needs a solver mode that integrates the full right-hand side.

  • Scheduling by backlog. DataBacklogPolicy(; horizon_s) scores a data link by what it could move in the next horizon_s seconds, min(c, stored_tx / horizon_s + production_tx, room_rx / horizon_s + outflow_rx) in bit/s, with c the link's rate and production_tx the transmitter's current source production rate. outflow_rx is the receiver's currently carried outgoing rate, sampled once from previously active links that remain available at the scheduling step, before choosing the new matching. It uses the runtime flow caps; it does not assume links in the new matching will be active. This is a scoring estimate only: actual transfers still obey the store's flow limits. It samples production at the current state and time and estimates that rate continuing over the horizon; it does not predict future source changes or incoming relay traffic. An empty sender that is still producing can keep its link active. A sender with neither stored data nor production, or a receiver with neither free space nor outflow, scores zero. A transmitter without a store counts as always holding data, and a receiver without one as always having room. It shares DataThroughputPolicy's unit, so the two mix in a WeightedPolicy without rescaling. A policy of your own reads the stores with stored_bits(ctx, i) (NaN without a store) and store_room_bits(ctx, i) (Inf without one).

  • Backlog scoring limits in relay networks. The score estimates a local transfer, not additional data delivered to its final destination. A full relay can attract incoming traffic that displaces its own production while a better direct path goes unused. Two full stores can also keep exchanging data without increasing delivery to a destination. Each incoming candidate independently receives the receiver's whole carried-outflow credit; that credit is neither reduced by the receiver's own production nor divided among candidates. If several links are selected, actual transfers still share the available flow capacity and respect store limits. These scoring limitations remain in v0.7.1; compare delivered and lost data when choosing a policy for a relay network.

  • Columns. A run with a store adds data_stored_bits, data_store_fill (the fraction of the capacity held), data_produced_bits and data_lost_bits per spacecraft (see the storage columns). data_rate_tx_bps and data_rate_rx_bps show the rates the links carry.

  • Not yet modelled. One store holds one stream of data: there are no priority classes, no queues per destination, and no age or latency of the data held.

Ground stations

A ground station is a fixed point on the rotating planet. Its antennas are link terminals:

dish = RadioTerminal(boresight=[0.0, 0.0, 1.0], max_power_w=20.0, gain_dbi=30.0, efficiency=0.5,
    noise_temperature_k=150.0)
station = GroundStation(name="north", lat_deg=42.3, lon_deg=-83.7, alt_m=270.0, min_elevation_deg=10.0,
    antennas=[dish])
s = add_ground_station!(model, station)                      # 1 for the first station
register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, sender), (-s, 1), link)  # a downlink
# or a link from every spacecraft to every station:
register_links!(model, spacecraft, station_pairs(spacecraft, model), link)
  • Nodes. Station s is the node -s, so its antenna k is the endpoint (-s, k) in link keys, the history, the contact windows and the link schedule. A link joins a spacecraft and a station; two stations cannot link. forbidden_pairs may name (i, -s).
  • Where it is. The station sits at its geodetic latitude, longitude and altitude on the planet's ellipsoid, and turns with the planet. Its inertial position is L_PIᵀ r and its velocity L_PIᵀ (ω × r), with r its planet-fixed position, L_PI the planet's frame and ω its spin.
    • Availability and the scheduling policies read the frame at each accepted step.
    • The rates read it at the time of each right-hand-side evaluation, so a station moves through a step instead of holding its place at the step's start.
  • Availability. A link to a station is available while the spacecraft is at least min_elevation_deg above the station's local horizon, the plane normal to its geodetic up direction, and within the link's range_m. The mask is the line of sight: a DataLink does not test the segment to a station against the planet.
  • Antennas. They are RadioTerminals, numbered 1:length(antennas), and carry one active link at a time. They steer freely above the mask, so their boresight is not read. A station has no power budget: its antennas draw nothing and are never shed.
  • Data. A downlink counts in its spacecraft's data_sent_bits. An uplink, a link from (-s, k) to a spacecraft's radio, counts in that spacecraft's data_received_bits. In a run with data links, each station also counts the bits it has received and sent, two integrated states (u.gs[s].received_bits and u.gs[s].sent_bits) written as the gs{s}_received_bits and gs{s}_sent_bits columns (see the data columns). They continue across a checkpoint resume.
  • With data stores. A station is a store with unbounded capacity and supply, so it never limits a link: a downlink carries what its spacecraft's store sends, and an uplink what its spacecraft's store can take in. stored_bits(ctx, -s) and store_room_bits(ctx, -s) are Inf, so DataBacklogPolicy drains the fullest stores to the ground first.
  • Scheduling. Station links are candidates like any other. A station is never an orbital policy's target. It has no battery, so its state_of_charge is NaN and EnergyBalancingPolicy scores a link to it 0.
  • Outputs. In the link schedule, a station end shows as -s in a_spacecraft or b_spacecraft, and by its registered name in a_name or b_name. These name columns are present only for station runs. Saved scenes include each site's planet-fixed coordinates; the station-capable Visualizer draws green markers and names on the rotating globe, and active or contact links in either direction. The labels toggle controls names and the interlinks toggle controls both stations and their links. Existing spacecraft-only scenes and schedule tables retain their format.
  • Checkpoints. A run with stations records each station's site, mask and antennas, and refuses to resume when any of them has changed.
  • Not yet modelled. Terrain and horizon masks, antenna steering limits and patterns, and atmospheric losses.

Scheduling

At the start of the run and after every accepted solver step, the engine marks a candidate available when its spacecraft pair is not forbidden, both spacecraft are active, neither terminal is shed, and its link's link_available allows it. The run's scheduling policy then scores each available link, and the scheduler selects the terminal matching with the largest total score. Only links with a positive score can be selected. The selected schedule is held during trial stages, which avoids changing it during rejected steps. An inactive endpoint contributes no force even if an earlier schedule still names its link.

In a run with interlinks, every spacecraft has two more integrated states. interlink_dv integrates the acceleration its selected links' forces give (m/s, inertial). interlink_delta_sma integrates their contribution to the instantaneous osculating semimajor-axis rate (m); it is not a comparison against a separately propagated unforced trajectory. Both stay zero for links that apply no force, such as power beams and data links. They are saved in the interlink columns. The history belongs to the returned run configuration. With the default state isolation, the input configuration is unchanged.

Policies

A scheduling policy is a value of a policy type. Give the run one with SimulationConfiguration(scheduling_policy_model=policy); the default is SemimajorAxisRatePolicy(). The run keeps it in a SchedulingPolicyModel, the scheduler, which also holds the matching workspace: args.scheduling_policy_model.objective is the policy.

The orbital policies take each link's force from its link_force. For SemimajorAxisRatePolicy, targets is a spacecraft vector index, a vector or range of them, :both (both ends of every link) or :lower_index (the lower spacecraft index of each pair). For EccentricityRatePolicy it is an index or :both. A link that lowers the objective scores negative, so it is never selected.

Energy balancing

EnergyBalancingPolicy() charges the lowest state of charge. It scores a power beam into terminal hardware, between two spacecraft with a battery, by the power it would deliver times the gap between the two batteries' states of charge, P × (s_tx − s_rx). So:

  • power flows only from a fuller battery to an emptier one, and a beam into a full battery never scores: a battery the power system counts as full (its stored energy within 1e-9 of its capacity) counts as 1, so two full batteries tie at 0;
  • of receivers competing for one sender's terminal, the emptier one wins at equal delivered power, while a much better-coupled receiver can still win over a slightly emptier one;
  • the score is signed: a beam from an emptier battery to a fuller one scores negative, so it is never selected alone and counts against the beam in a mix;
  • a link with no battery at either end, or one that would deliver no power, scores 0.

The selection changes only at accepted steps, so when two states of charge cross within a step, the beam runs to the step's end. PowerDeliveryPolicy, by contrast, beams into a full battery: the receiver curtails, and the sender still pays.

Weighted mixes

WeightedPolicy(PolicyTerm(policy; scale, weight=1.0)...) scores each link by Σ weight × (score / scale) over its terms, summed in term order. scale is in the term's own unit, the amount of its score that counts as one, so each term is dimensionless before the weights apply; weight is finite and may be negative. The scales are fixed, not normalized by each step's best link, so a link's score does not depend on the other candidates.

policy = WeightedPolicy(
    PolicyTerm(SemimajorAxisRatePolicy(targets=1); scale=1.0),   # 1 m/s of SMA rate counts as one
    PolicyTerm(EnergyBalancingPolicy(); scale=100.0))            # and so do 100 W
args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model, scheduling_policy_model=policy)

Each term applies its own rules: an orbital term scores power beams 0 and a power term scores momentum links 0, so a mix weighs link kinds against each other. Each term's checks run, and an eccentricity term keeps its near-circular refusal over the mix's candidates. Mixes nest.

A custom policy

A policy type subtypes AbstractSchedulingPolicy and implements candidate_score. This one prefers short links:

struct ShortLinkPolicy <: SpaceAGORA.AbstractSchedulingPolicy end
SpaceAGORA.candidate_score(::ShortLinkPolicy, candidate, ctx, t) =
    1 / sqrt(sum(abs2, candidate.a.pos_ii - candidate.b.pos_ii))

args = SimulationConfiguration(...; interlink_model=model, scheduling_policy_model=ShortLinkPolicy())
  • The candidate (LinkCandidate) has its key, its link, its endpoints' state samples a and b (in key order, so a directed link's transmitter is a), and the terminal hardware at its ends, terminal_a and terminal_b (nothing for a virtual terminal).
  • The context (SchedulingContext) has the planet's gravitational parameter mu, the link_context to pass to the link hooks, and the run's spacecraft; state_of_charge(ctx, i) gives spacecraft i's battery state of charge (NaN without a battery), and stored_bits(ctx, i) and store_room_bits(ctx, i) the data its store holds and the room left (NaN and Inf without a store). t is the elapsed time in seconds.
  • The rules. The hook is called for available candidates only; the others score 0. A nonfinite score is refused, and only positive scores can be selected. A run refuses at setup a policy type without a candidate_score method.
  • Plain values. A policy holds no run state: it is a value whose fields say what it scores. A checkpoint records its type and every field by value (numbers with their bits; arrays, tuples, named tuples and structs element by element and field by field), so an equal policy rebuilt for the resume resumes, and a resume with another value is refused. A function field is recorded by its type and, for a closure or a callable struct, every value it holds; a named function is recorded by its type alone, so a change to its code is not detected. A closure reads the globals it uses when it is called, and their values are not recorded: build it in a function or a let block, so that it captures them. A checkpointed run refuses at setup what a checkpoint cannot record by value: a closure that captures a variable reassigned after the closure was made, a dictionary or a set, a stream, a task or a lock, a pointer or a value holding one (such as a Regex), whose address differs from one process to the next, an unassigned field or element, and fields that contain themselves.

Contact windows

The history (model.history) has one InterLinkHistory sample per accepted step: its time, the links the scheduler selected (active) and the links that were available (available). The available links are recorded in a run with a data link, or when you ask for them with InterlinkModel(; record_available=true); record_available=false leaves them out. In a large constellation over a long run they are a list per step, so other runs leave them out by default. contact_windows(history) turns it into windows, one (key, start_s, end_s, duration_s) for each span over which a link could close; contact_windows(history; active=true) gives the spans over which it was selected. contact_time_s(history, key) sums a link's windows.

solution = run_simulation(args; return_solution=true)
history = solution.prob.p.args.interlink_model.history
windows = contact_windows(history)
contact_time_s(history, ((1, 1), (2, 1)); active=true)

A window starts at the first accepted step that has the link and ends at the next step that does not, or at the last step: the resolution is the scheduler's. A checkpointed run keeps its history in simulation_checkpoint.interlinks.bin beside its checkpoint, and a resumed run takes it back, so a resumed history holds the whole run, as the results do (see Outputs). If that record is missing or unreadable, the run warns and records only the resumed interval. A resume that solves nothing returns an empty schedule in that case. With the same configuration, checkpoint schedule and deterministic models whose complete run state is restored, resumed history matches the uninterrupted checkpointed run. A run without checkpoints can take different adaptive steps and record different scheduling samples.

The link schedule

Each history sample also records the score that selected each active link (scores), read from the scheduler after it selects. link_schedule turns the history into a table with one row per window: a span over which a link was selected (window == "active") and, when the history records the available links, a span over which a link could close ("contact"). Each row gives the link (its number, kind, direction and endpoints), the span (start_s, end_s, duration_s and the number of samples, steps) and, for an active row, the score at its first and last sample and its extremes.

results = run_simulation(args; return_results=true)
schedule = link_schedule(results)              # or link_schedule(model; policy=scheduler)
active = schedule[schedule.window .== "active", :]

The scores are in the policy's unit (see Policies): m/s for SemimajorAxisRatePolicy, W for the power policies, bit/s for DataThroughputPolicy, 1 for a WeightedPolicy. The table's metadata names the policy and the unit. With InterlinkModel(; record_terms=true) and a WeightedPolicy, each sample also records each top-level term's own score, in its own unit, and the table gains term1_start, term2_start, ...: each term's score at the span's first sample. A run with another policy refuses record_terms=true. Recording the terms leaves the mix's scores, and so the schedule, unchanged.

A run that writes results writes the table beside them, as simulation_results_link_schedule.csv (when save_csv is set) and simulation_results_link_schedule.feather, and lists both in the results manifest with the policy and its unit (see the link schedule's columns). The table is not thinned with the results rows. A run without interlinks writes none, and link_schedule(results) returns the table's columns with no rows. Like the contact windows, the schedule has the scheduler's resolution. A resumed run restores its recorded history from beside the checkpoint, including earlier windows. Its metadata and manifest give history_start_s and history_end_s. If the history is unavailable, a warning identifies the missing earlier interval; that interval means not recorded, rather than no link. With save_visualization_scene, the viewer payload carries the schedule's windows, so the viewer can draw each link while it is active (see the viewer page).

Optional matching backend

Tree-shaped components are solved in Julia. Of parallel links (directed links both ways between the same two terminals), only the higher-scoring one is a candidate, so a two-way pair alone is a tree. Cyclic components use NetworkX's maximum-weight matching through the optional PythonCall extension. Core loading and tree matching do not initialize Python. A cyclic component without the backend produces an installation error rather than using an approximate match.

Install PythonCall in the Julia environment used for the run, and install NetworkX (>=3.4,<4) in a compatible Python environment. Set JULIA_PYTHONCALL_EXE to that interpreter before starting Julia; when managing Python yourself, also set JULIA_CONDAPKG_BACKEND=Null. Then explicitly load PythonCall and SpaceAGORA in either order. For example, in a separate Julia project containing SpaceAGORA:

using Pkg
Pkg.add("PythonCall")
using SpaceAGORA, PythonCall

The backend requires Python's global interpreter lock. Run cyclic matching on its owning thread, or use independent process workers. Merely adding more Julia threads does not make arbitrary calls into Python safe. Both matching paths use exact integer weights derived from the floating-point scores. Equal-score ties may choose different valid optima across backend versions.

A custom link kind

A kind implements link_range_m and the effects it has. This one pulls its endpoints together with a constant force:

struct PullLink <: SpaceAGORA.AbstractInterlink
    force_n::Float64
    range_m::Float64
end
SpaceAGORA.link_range_m(link::PullLink) = link.range_m
function SpaceAGORA.link_force(link::PullLink, a, b, ctx)
    direction = (b.pos_ii - a.pos_ii) / sqrt(sum(abs2, b.pos_ii - a.pos_ii))
    return (link.force_n * direction, -link.force_n * direction)
end

register_link!(model, spacecraft, (1, 1), (2, 1), PullLink(3.0, 2_000.0))

A run refuses at setup a link whose kind has no link_range_m method. Links of different kinds can be registered in one model.

At a ground station end (see Ground stations), the hooks receive the station's state sample: the station is its spacecraft, its spacecraft_index is -s, and it has no mass and no attitude. The station sits on the planet, so a kind that tests the planet's occultation should leave a station end to the station's elevation mask, as DataLink does (a.spacecraft isa GroundStation). A station's antennas are RadioTerminals, which carry a kind only where terminal_supports allows it.

Terminals and torques

A kind that reads its terminal hardware, or that turns the spacecraft it links, implements the terminal-aware forms of the hooks. The engine calls these forms, and their defaults call the ones above, so a kind written without them works as before, bit for bit.

  • link_available(link, terminal_a, terminal_b, a, b, ctx) decides whether the link can close, given the terminal hardware at its two ends (nothing for a virtual terminal).
  • interlink_wrench(link, terminal_a, terminal_b, a, b, ctx) returns (force_a_ii, torque_a_body, force_b_ii, torque_b_body).
    • The forces act as link_force's do, and the scheduling policies score candidate links by them.
    • Each torque is in its spacecraft's body frame, about its body reference point, as a mounted actuator's torque is (see Frames).
    • The engine adds each torque to its spacecraft's angular acceleration as I⁻¹ τ, with I the inertia tensor. This is exact for the rigid spacecraft interlinks support.
  • link_applies_torque(link) = true says that a kind's torques can be nonzero. A run with such a link needs attitude (orientation_sim=true), and setup refuses it otherwise. A nonzero torque from a kind that does not say so stops a run without attitude.
  • terminal_pose(spacecraft, terminal) gives a mounted terminal's boresight and location in its spacecraft's body frame. A force applied at that location has the torque the terminal's own recoil would have: the cross product of the location and the force, in the body frame.

Supported scope

Interlinks apply forces, and through interlink_wrench torques, to rigid spacecraft, or carry power or data. The laser momentum link does not model terminal pointing, occultation, beam losses or torques; LaserBeamLink adds them as options (see Laser physics). Neither models the terminals' thermal dynamics. Links through hardware terminals draw battery power and close when it is shed; links between virtual terminals draw none. Actuator faults on a link-driven terminal do not yet act on its link. The spacecraft's battery_energy_index and tempurature_index fields are metadata, not availability constraints. The cavity magnification and force law are modeling assumptions; passing implementation tests does not validate a physical laser system.

Use a full-state solver such as :tsit5, or the opt-in :multirate_mri route described in Solver configuration. The original split modes (:split_imex, :multirate, :gravity_backbone_split), external propagator coupling, articulated/compliant vehicles and independent constellation ensembles are rejected for linked spacecraft. Near circularity, EccentricityRatePolicy also rejects candidate sets that can simultaneously apply several positive-score links to one target: individual eccentricity-rate norms are not additive there. SemimajorAxisRatePolicy does not have that restriction.

Scheduling follows accepted steps, so changes of range or preferred link are resolved at that cadence. Check timestep convergence for a new case. A checkpoint resume must retain the interlink topology, link kinds (each named by its module and type, with its concrete type parameters) and parameters, scheduling policy (its type and every field), and each spacecraft's terminal hardware: its kinds, in mounting order, and every field of each, such as a receiver's aperture or a transmitter's efficiency; a new run is required when those change. Checkpoints written by SpaceAGORA 0.4 and earlier still resume, and so do checkpoints written with the symbol policies of 0.5, under the typed policy that schedules the same. A resumed run takes its interlink history back from beside its checkpoint, as its results take back their rows, so its history, schedule and results table hold the whole run, and integrated diagnostic states continue from the checkpoint. Restoring recorded history requires the same record_available and record_terms settings; changing either is refused before result streams are changed. Start a new run to change these settings.
