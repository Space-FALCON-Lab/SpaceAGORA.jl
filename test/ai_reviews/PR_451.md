What it does:
- A directed power beam (`PowerBeamLink`) from a `LaserTerminal` to a new `PowerReceiver` terminal, with a Gaussian beam and planet occultation.
- The delivered power is generation in the receiver's power budget.
- The beam is a tier-1 load at the sender.
- A side-aware `terminal_supports`, a `:power_delivery` policy, and two columns.

Rule: runs without a power beam or a receiver are bitwise unchanged.

- **The beam** (`power_links.jl`):
  - The spot radius is `w0 √(1 + (d/z_R)²)`, with `z_R = π w0²/λ`, and the
    captured fraction is `1 − exp(−2r²/w²)`, computed with `expm1`.
  - The delivered power is `min(efficiency × captured, max_power_w)`.
  - With the defaults (1.064 µm, 5 cm waist) and a 25 cm-radius receiver, an
    independent recomputation gives `z_R` ≈ 7.38 km, and captures of about 1.0 at
    10 km, 0.237 at 100 km and 0.0027 at 1000 km, matching the design.
- **Occultation.** `_segment_occulted` finds the point of the segment between the
  two spacecraft closest to the planet's centre, clamped to the segment, and
  compares its distance with `Rp_e`. Without a planet it returns false.
- **Direction.** The side-aware `terminal_supports` lets a `LaserTerminal`
  transmit but not receive a power beam, and a `PowerReceiver` receive but carry
  no other link kind (D-I3-6).
- **The energy balance.** All three power-snapshot callers that feed the battery
  or the saved columns pass the full run state. The co-orbit test checks the
  battery's change against the integral of its net-power column. A mutant that
  drops the state at the right-hand-side call is caught.
- **The main merge.** `ea932c81e` (main `6183d00d2`) has the tree `git merge-tree`
  writes for its parents. The branch merges cleanly with main `6d77179ba`, #449
  and #450.
- No commit carries a trailer, and `## Changed Files` lists all 12 `src` files.
- **The P1 fix** (`263a91004`, reviewed the same way):
  - `_interlink_terminal_identity` records the kind and every field through
    `_interlink_kind_parameter`, the encoder the link-kind identity already
    uses. So floats carry their bits, `Inf` included, and an `SVector` is
    recorded field by field.
  - `LaserTerminal` and `PowerReceiver` are immutable and hold only
    configuration, so the identity cannot pick up run state.
  - The key is still added only when a spacecraft has terminal hardware.
  - Low: a user's terminal kind with a non-bits field is compared by value, as
    link fields are; a field holding run state would refuse an unchanged resume.
  - The branch at `263a91004` merges cleanly with main `d3288ce8d` and with every

## Findings

One P1, from review, fixed in 263a91004 (below). No other P1 or P2 findings.

- **2026-10-20, review thread "Include receiver parameters in checkpoint identity" (P1, fixed in 263a91004).**
  - The problem: the interlink checkpoint configuration named each hardware terminal by its kind only (`resume_checkpoint.jl`). A resume with the same links but a rebuilt `PowerReceiver` of another `radius_m`, `efficiency` or `max_power_w` passed the check, and gave different received power and battery results. A `LaserTerminal` of another `efficiency` passed it too, with a different link-driven draw.
  - The fix: each terminal is now recorded by its kind and every one of its fields, encoded as type parameters are: a float with its bits (`Inf` included), an `SVector` field by field. That covers what changes a link-driven terminal:
    - a receiver's aperture, efficiency and limit;
    - a laser terminal's efficiency (its draw) and `max_power_w` (the setup limit check).

    A laser terminal's `boresight` and `location` matter only to a controller-driven terminal, which is no link endpoint. Its `magnification` and `range_m` matter only through `LaserMomentumLink(terminal)`, whose fields the link identity already records. All fields are recorded anyway, so one rule covers every terminal kind, I-4's `RadioTerminal` included, and the pointing models to come.
  - The key still appears only when the run has terminal hardware, so runs on virtual terminals keep their checkpoint configuration unchanged.
  - Link fields are still compared by value (`isequal`), as checkpoints since 0.4 were written; only terminal fields use the bits encoding.
  - Low: a checkpoint written by main between #444 and this fix names its terminals by kind only, so it is refused on resume, with the configuration-mismatch message. No release has `hardware_terminals` (#444 merged after v0.5.0).

Points a reviewer should check:

- **Bitwise identity.** Every new path is gated:
  - the snapshot adds received power only when `link_receives` (a directed link ends on that spacecraft's hardware);
  - the two columns exist only with a `PowerBeamLink`;
  - `has_power_parts` changes only with a `PowerReceiver`;
  - the three-argument `terminal_supports` falls back to the two-argument form for existing kinds, so their registrations accept and refuse as before.
  The laser examples and fingerprints below confirm it.
- **Low, by design: the receiver mounts as a user-defined kind.** `PowerReceiver` goes through the generic `add_actuator!` path (`link.custom_actuators`), so every per-actuator loop handles it unchanged. The mounting order's convention puts built-in kinds before user-defined ones; a dedicated list would need every mounting loop changed. It holds no level, so its position only moves later user-defined actuators by one.
- **Low, by design: a full receiver curtails** (D-I3-5). The sender keeps paying for a beam its receiver cannot store; I-5's energy balancing is where that is avoided.
- **Low: grazing rays.** A beam path that only touches the planet's sphere is blocked. The test is against the equatorial radius, so it is conservative near the poles of an oblate body.
- **Low: one policy per run.** `:power_delivery` scores only power delivery, and the force policies score only force. A run mixing momentum and power links waits for I-5.
- **The run state in the snapshot.** `_power_snapshot` reads the transmitter's
  state, through its `state` keyword, to evaluate a received beam. Every caller
  that feeds the battery or the columns passes the full run state:
  - `_apply_power_rhs!` (the right-hand side's power rate);
  - `_update_power_states!` (the step-end shedding and flag update);
  - `_save_power` (`power_generated_w`, the net and received columns).

  So the battery integrates the same generation the columns report. The
  default (`nothing`, no received power) covers only callers that cannot
  involve a beam. The other endpoint's state is only read, so this is safe in
  the threaded per-spacecraft right-hand side. A mutant that drops the state at
  the right-hand-side call is caught by the energy-integral check.
- **Overlaps.** `power_callbacks.jl` (the snapshot's `state` keyword, its callers and the two columns) and `interlink_scheduling.jl` (one policy branch). These were announced to the coordinator before the edit. `control_callbacks.jl`, `callbacks/assembly.jl`, the callback list and `simulation_model.jl` are untouched.

  - The beam:
    - the spot radius and capture fraction against the closed form at six ranges, including `d = 0`, `d = z_R` and `d ≫ z_R`;
    - the 100 km and 1000 km captures;
    - directed, levels `(power_w, 0)`, no force, and validation;

    - the key, transmitter first;
    - a receiver refused as transmitter (its message names the side), two receivers refused, and a laser terminal refused as receiver;

    - the side-aware trait.
  - Occultation:
    - near spacecraft available; a path through the Earth unavailable; and available with no planet;
    - a tangent ray blocked, a clear one available;
    - coincident and out-of-range endpoints unavailable.
  - Energy balance over 60 s, on a co-orbit 5 km apart:
    - the sender drops by `power_w / efficiency × t`;
    - the receiver rises by `power_w × capture × efficiency × t`;
    - the received, generated, beamed and load columns agree;
    - each battery's change equals the integral of its net-power column (c33471515).
  - A full receiver: its battery stays full, the sender still pays, and received power still shows.
  - Shedding at the sender: a 0.05 Wh sender battery closes the beam between 0.5 and 2 s and keeps it closed; the receiver gains only up to then.
  - Scheduling: `:gve_sma` never selects the beam, `:power_delivery` holds it, and out of range nothing is selected.
  - Columns: no `power_received`/`power_beamed` columns in a laser momentum run, and a receiver alone counts as a power part.
  - Checkpoints:
    - the unchanged configuration resumes;
    - changed beam power, wavelength or waist is refused with the state untouched;
    - an added receiver is refused;
    - the receiver's module-path identity is in `hardware_terminals`; since 263a91004, with every field, `max_power_w = Inf` with its bits.
  - Checkpoints record each terminal's fields (263a91004), through the checkpoint file of a 30 s first leg:
    - a rebuilt receiver with another radius, efficiency or limit, and a transmitter with another efficiency, are each refused;
    - the refusal comes before the activity flags are restored;
    - the full resume through `run_simulation` is refused too;
    - the unchanged run then resumes from the same file and ends with the same batteries as a straight run.
- `test/unit/simulation/interlink_terminal_tests.jl` (263a91004): the laser terminal's identity has every field, its efficiency with its bits; a run on virtual terminals has no `hardware_terminals`.
