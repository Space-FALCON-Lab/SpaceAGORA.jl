## Findings

Three failed or interrupted attempts are retained. An RNG-only repair still produced different repeated plans at four threads; shared temporary bindings in cost evaluation were also racing. Isolating them restores exact one/four-thread snapshots. The first uncapped full ISS run had 20 sampled keep-out crossings and 2.95 m arrival error despite a clear geometric path. Its reference started at 0.569 m/s from a stationary spacecraft and contained a 1.185 m/s² discrete speed change, above its 0.009615 m/s² initial per-axis thrust authority. The final demonstration uses the identical geometric path and cost with a 0.1 m/s reference cap; it passes the same acceptance thresholds. A separate station-frame check found a fixed inertial attitude inconsistent with the RTN geometry; the circular station now rotates at its orbital rate.

The speed-only validation was stopped before its full run so the final attitude correction could be validated together. No source failure is inferred from that interrupted attempt.

## Residual Risk

The geometric retimer does not guarantee a full acceleration profile from rest, and LQ-MPC does not impose collision constraints. The bounded demonstration is validated against its sampled station point cloud, not operational ISS geometry or a continuous collision certificate. Arbitrary tumbling targets, different orbits/configurations, video export, full Linux CI and performance gains are not established. Native GRAM is not used; the executed simulations use SPICE and no atmosphere.

Seeded plans change from older versions. Fixed iteration budgets support reproducibility across thread counts; wall-clock stopping criteria do not. Controller copying preserves stored primal state but rebuilds native factorization, dual and diagnostic caches, so it is not an exact optimizer checkpoint. The final documentation-only follow-up is separate from the executed behavior candidate.
