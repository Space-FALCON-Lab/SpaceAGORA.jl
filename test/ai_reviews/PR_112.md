# AI Review Artifact — PR #112

## Scope
Re-anchor the Odyssey telemetry replay to the NAV reconstruction after each trim burn, through a new engine mechanism (scheduled state anchors) and a new manifest block. The sentinel's densities, truth series, comparison axis and thresholds are unchanged; only the propagation protocol changes.

- src/simulation/engine/state_access.jl — new `_set_state_position_velocity_ii!(u, sat_idx, pos, vel)` for the first-order ComponentVector layout; it throws for the gravity-backbone ArrayPartition layout (see the findings).

- src/SpaceAGORA.jl — imports `StateAnchor` and `get_state_anchor_callback` from `SimulationModel`, exports them, and forwards their docstrings onto the root bindings with the existing `@doc` pattern (the docs build resolves `@docs SpaceAGORA.X` against the root's own metadata only; the first CI docs run failed on exactly that).

## Findings
- P2: an anchor is a discontinuity in the state; the orbit-count callback detects apoapsis on the sign change of the radial velocity. Checked: every Odyssey anchor sits after the apoapsis passage plus the replayed burn's duration plus 600 s, on the post-apoapsis side, and the anchored kernel state is also post-apoapsis within seconds of the propagated one, so no crossing is created or skipped. The probe confirms the anchor is hit as a tstop and the count of saved steps continues.
- P2: the replayed burn and the anchor could double-count the burn. Checked: the burn is replayed first (finite burn at 4 N from the sim's apoapsis) and the anchor, taken after the burn's duration, then supersedes the state; the burn's effect on mass remains, which matches the mission. Anchoring before the burn ended would have replaced a mid-burn state with a post-burn one and lost part of the burn, which is why the walk-out raise (a 38-minute burn ending too close to the next periapsis) is not anchored.
- P2: the tabulated-flight density model picks the pass by nearest periapsis time; the anchors keep the sim's timing aligned with the kernel's, which is also what keeps the pass selection correct late in the campaign.
- P2: under the gravity-backbone split (a `SecondOrderODEProblem` stepped by KahanLi8) an in-place state edit reaches the integrator's arrays (verified by re-reading them inside the affect and five seconds later) but the stepper then ends the solve at the next tstop with a success return code, which would truncate a run silently. Anchors are therefore refused at callback initialisation under that solver mode with an explicit error; the Odyssey sentinel runs on the default first-order path. Covered by the probe.
- P2 (Codex): several anchors at the same instant (one per satellite) were applied one per callback invocation, which fires once per solver time, so the second and everything after it would have been skipped. The affect now applies every anchor within the time tolerance in one invocation; the probe anchors twice at one instant and once later and checks all three fire and the trajectory follows the last one.
- P2 (Codex): the staged density callback runs before the extra callbacks and records an atmosphere sample for the pre-anchor state at the anchor time; the buffered reader (exact-time match) and the freeze-per-step reader would hand it to the next RHS evaluation. The affect clears the satellite's `density_sample_t` and `in_atmosphere_sample_t`, which makes every reader resample at the anchored position. The Odyssey anchors sit near apoapsis where the density is nil, so this matters for other uses of the mechanism rather than for the sentinel.
- P2 (from the first nightly run of this PR): the propagated trajectory drifts along-track by hundreds to thousands of kilometres within a segment (the model's per-pass decay error becomes a period error), so by the late campaign the sim could be tens of minutes out of phase when an anchor fired. An anchor placed after the kernel's apoapsis then moved a sim that was still before its own apoapsis across that crossing, the orbit counter fell one behind, every later burn fired one orbit late and no longer matched its anchor, and the run slowed to minutes per orbit with mis-timed burns. `StateAnchor` now carries an optional `orbit_count` that the affect writes into the satellite's orbit counter; the harness derives it as B - offset + 1 from the burn's mission number and the maneuver offset, which is the counter's value between that burn's apoapsis and the next. Covered by the probe (a three-orbit mission anchored with count 3 terminates at the next apoapsis) and the parser testset (counts 8 and 15 for burns 25 and 32 with offset 18).
- P3: `sol(t_anchor)` evaluates to the pre-anchor state because the save precedes the affect; consumers that read the solution at the anchor time itself see the propagated state, and the next saved point sees the anchored one. Documented in the probe.
- P3: the manifest block stores the anchor states inline (six numbers each, 25 anchors) rather than as a data file, matching how `initial_state_j2000_m` is stored and avoiding a new tracked data artifact; the generator script reproduces the block from the NAF kernel.

- P2 (runtime): the anchored replay follows the real campaign into its densest passes, and under the default `auto_stiff` sequence the Rosenbrock fallback with finite-difference Jacobians turned pathological in the final ten orbits after the walk-out raise (51 minutes locally for ten orbits that plain Tsit5 integrates in under a minute); the hosted runner cancelled the job at its 240-minute limit twice, and every main-based Odyssey run since the #108 head has been cancelled the same way. Resolved in `.github/workflows/nightly-stress.yml`: the Odyssey matrix entry pins `SPACEAGORA_TELEMETRY_SOLVER_MODE=tsit5` (metrics identical to the fourth digit locally, whole profile about 60% faster) and the job limit is 360 minutes; VEx and Earth keep the default sequence.

## Residual Risk
Low to medium. The mechanism is small and covered; the decisive check is the full Odyssey profile on the nightly job. If a segment between two anchors still crosses a threshold, that is a model error in that segment and exactly what the sentinel exists to show. The anchor epochs assume the manifest's burn list and P-numbering; regenerating them after any change to either is a documented script run.
