Powering a Spacecraft

A spacecraft can carry a power budget: solar arrays that generate, a battery that stores, and loads that draw. The mounted actuators draw the power they report through actuator_consumption (laser terminals, and reaction wheels and torque rods given power figures). When a battery is mounted, its stored energy is part of the integrated state, and when it runs empty the engine sheds actuator load until the battery has recharged.

The parts

PartMounted onWhat it does
SolarArray(; normal, area_m2, efficiency, max_power_w=Inf)any linkgenerates area_m2 * efficiency * S * max(0, cos θ) * ν W, where S is the solar flux, θ the angle between normal (link frame) and the Sun, and ν the lit fraction of the Sun's disk
Battery(; capacity_wh, initial_soc, charge_efficiency, discharge_efficiency, max_charge_w, max_discharge_w, recovery_soc=0.05)the root link, at most onestores the surplus and covers a shortfall
PowerLoad(; power_w, name)any linka constant load, never shed

Mount them with add_power!(spacecraft, link, part). An array on a hinged panel turns with the panel. Without initial_soc, the battery starts at the spacecraft's battery_energy_index (a percentage).

What happens during a run

  • Generation. The Sun's direction comes from the run's ephemerides: SPICE, or, with SimpleEphemeridesModel, a low-precision model for Earth (other bodies need SPICE). The solar flux uses the central body's mean distance from the Sun (1 AU for Earth and the Moon); the eclipse model is the one of solar radiation pressure.
  • Charging. A surplus charges the battery at charge_efficiency, up to max_charge_w; a full battery curtails the rest.
  • Discharging. A shortfall discharges it over discharge_efficiency, up to max_discharge_w.
  • Shedding. When the battery is empty, the engine sheds actuator load in tiers until what is left fits what the arrays generate: laser terminals first, then torque rods, then the reaction wheels' motors (a shed wheel puts no torque on the vehicle). Shedding ends once the battery has recharged to recovery_soc of its capacity. Constant loads are never shed; while the battery is empty and they exceed the generation, the net power stays negative.

The battery's full and empty states switch at events, when its stored energy reaches its capacity or zero; the shedding tier is updated at the end of each integration step. The split solver modes (:split_imex, :multirate, :gravity_backbone_split) refuse a spacecraft with a battery. A checkpointed run keeps these states from one segment to the next, but a run with a battery cannot resume from a checkpoint file: the file holds the stored energy, not whether the battery is empty and recharging.

A small example

using SpaceAGORA, StaticArrays
bus = Link(root=true, m=10.0, dims=MVector(0.1, 0.2, 0.3))
spacecraft = SpacecraftModel(links=[bus], root=bus, inertia_tensor=bus.inertia)
add_power!(spacecraft, bus, SolarArray(normal=SVector(1.0, 0.0, 0.0), area_m2=0.06, efficiency=0.3))
add_power!(spacecraft, bus, Battery(capacity_wh=5.0, initial_soc=0.5))
add_power!(spacecraft, bus, PowerLoad(power_w=5.0, name="avionics"))
add_actuator!(spacecraft, bus, LaserTerminal(boresight=SVector(1.0, 0.0, 0.0), max_power_w=2.0, efficiency=0.2))

examples/Earth_CubeSat_Power_Budget.jl flies this vehicle for two orbits with its laser on: the battery fills in sunlight, empties in eclipse, the laser is shed until the Sun returns, and the script checks each of these.

The results carry the power columns: stored energy, state of charge, generation, load, shed power and net power.
