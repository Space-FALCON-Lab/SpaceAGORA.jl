Why stacked: this branch changes `setup.jl`, `execution.jl` and `SpaceAGORA.jl`,

storage itself does not depend on the link schedule or the viewer.

What it does:
- A spacecraft may carry one `DataStore(; capacity_bits, initial_bits=0.0)` and
  any number of data sources, mounted with `add_data!`. `DataSource(; rate_bps)`
  produces a constant rate; a source kind of your own subtypes
  `AbstractDataSource` and implements `data_production_bps(source, x, ctx, t)`.
- The engine integrates `data_stored_bits`, `data_produced_bits` and
  `data_lost_bits`, which balance:
  `stored = initial + produced + received − sent − lost`.
- Flow control: a data link carries at most its `link_data_rate_bps`, and less
  at a store. An empty store passes what comes in on (its production and its
  incoming links), shared among its outgoing links in proportion to their
  rates; a full store takes in no more than it sends on, and its own production
  beyond that is lost. A spacecraft without a store neither limits nor is
  limited.
- Events mark a store full or empty, as a battery's do, with a step-end update
  of the flags; the flags continue across a checkpoint resume.
- Columns: `data_stored_bits`, `data_store_fill`, `data_produced_bits` and
  `data_lost_bits` per spacecraft; `data_rate_tx_bps` and `data_rate_rx_bps`
  show the carried rates.

Rule: a run without a data store is unchanged, bit for bit: its states, its
columns, its rates and its checkpoint payload.

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings in the author's own review. Points a reviewer should check:

- **F1, the flow-control solver.** `_carried_data_rates` starts every link at
  no flow and, in each pass, sets each link's rate from the previous pass's
  inflows and outflows: at most its rate; out of an empty store, the store's
  share of what comes in; into a full store, the store's share of what goes
  out. The shares grow with the flows, so the rates only grow, and every pass
  keeps within the stores' limits (an empty store never sends more than came in
  at the previous pass, which is no more than now). It stops when a pass
  changes nothing, or after one pass more than there are stores, which a chain
  of empty relays needs. So:
  - a chain converges exactly; a cycle of empty stores carries only what its
    sources feed it, never a circulation from nothing, and at the pass cap its
    rates are a feasible lower bound;
  - a run without any empty or full store skips the passes: each link carries
    its rate.
- **F2, priorities at a full store.** A full store's incoming links take
  precedence over its own production: they take what it sends on, and its
  production keeps only what is left, the rest lost. So links lose nothing
  (D-I8-3), and only production is lost (D-I8-5).
- **F3, the states of every spacecraft (a deviation from design §3.2).** The
  design added the store states only for spacecraft with a store, as
  `battery_wh` is. The run state needs one shape for every spacecraft, so a run
  with stores on some spacecraft only failed to build it. Every spacecraft of a
  run with a store carries the three states, and they stay zero for one
  without a store, whose storage columns are NaN. A battery on some spacecraft
  only has the same limit, unchanged here.
- **F4, rounding at the limits.** The shares can round an empty store's net
  rate a few ulps below zero, or a full one's above. An empty store's net rate
  is held at no less than zero and a full one's at no more, as a battery's
  rates are, so neither drifts past its limit and repeats its event.
- **F5, a pre-existing saving issue, not changed here.** A results row saved
  strictly inside an integration step that a boundary event cuts short can hold
  a value other than the solution's. Reproduced on #502's head with a battery
  alone: an empty event at 6.1 s in a step from 5.93 s; the row at 6.0 s holds
  0.0126 Wh where the solution holds 0.0082 Wh. The solution, the step-end
  states and the rows outside such steps are right. The store's events follow
  the battery's pattern, so a store's rows share it; the tests check the
  stored data through the solution. Worth its own follow-up.
- **F6, sources need a store.** A source on a spacecraft without a store is
  refused at setup, where the design left production without a store
  unspecified: there is nowhere to hold it, and counting it lost would hide a
  configuration error.
- **F7, checkpoints.** The callbacks' snapshot gains the stores' flags and
  their configuration only in a run with a store, so a run without one writes
  the snapshot it wrote before, and its old checkpoints resume. A run with a
  store refuses a snapshot without the stores, or with another capacity or
  initial content.
- **Low, by design: one stream.** One store holds one stream of data, with no
  priority classes (I-8c), no queues per destination and no age (D-I8-6,
  D-I8-8).

## Residual Risk

- **Saved rows inside an event step** (F5) can be off for the store, as they
  are for the battery, until the saving is fixed.
- **Per-evaluation cost.** A run with stores solves the flow control at every
  right-hand-side evaluation, over its active data links; it allocates a few
  vectors per evaluation and has not been measured on a large constellation.
- **Cycles of empty stores** are solved to a feasible lower bound at the pass
  cap rather than exactly (F1).
- **Production before the first event.** A store's flags come from its initial
  content; production into a store that starts exactly at its capacity is
  lost from the start.
