## Scope

`interlink-link-schedule` (#501).
- Plan: "Constellations and interlinks: implementation plan" (2026-10-19), I-9.

  planet" (2026-10-21), part I-9a.

  v0.6.1 tag.

Why stacked: this branch changes `interlink_graph.jl`, `interlink_scheduling.jl`,
`data_links.jl`, `setup.jl`, `results.jl` and `SpaceAGORA.jl`, which #501
changes too, and the viewer's interlink block (`viewer_payload.jl`), the
interlinks page, the public API list and the hook registry, which #501 or #502
change. The viewer block must leave station links out (F8).

What it does:
- `GroundStation(; name, lat_deg, lon_deg, alt_m=0.0, min_elevation_deg=10.0,
  antennas)` is a fixed point on the run's planet with `RadioTerminal` antennas.
  `add_ground_station!(model, station)` registers it on the `InterlinkModel` and
  returns its number `s`.
- Station `s` is the node `-s` (D-I9-1): its antenna `k` is the endpoint
  `(-s, k)`. `InterLinkKey`, the history, the contact windows, the link schedule
  and the checkpoint's endpoint encoding keep their types.
- `register_link!` and `register_links!` take station ends; `station_pairs`
  lists every spacecraft and station pair; two stations cannot link;
  `forbidden_pairs` may name `(i, -s)`.
- A station's state sample: `pos_ii = L_PIᵀ r_pp` and `vel_ii = L_PIᵀ (ω × r_pp)`
  from its geodetic position on the planet's ellipsoid
  (`Geodesy.geodetic_to_pp`), no mass and no attitude, the station as its model
  and `-s` as its index.
- Availability: the spacecraft at least `min_elevation_deg` above the station's
  local horizon, from its geodetic up direction, and the link kind's own
  `link_available`.
- The planet's frame (D-I9-4): availability and the policies read `planet.L_PI`
  at accepted steps; the force, data and received-power passes read the frame
  at each right-hand-side evaluation's time, and so do the saved rate columns.

Rule: a run without ground stations is unchanged, bit for bit: its states, its
schedule, its columns and its checkpoint configuration.

## Findings

No P1 or P2 findings in the author's own review. Points a reviewer should check:

- **F1, a design gap: the occultation test at a station end.** A `DataLink`
  (I-3's rule) is available only while the segment between its ends clears a
  sphere of the planet's equatorial radius. A station on the ellipsoid sits on
  that sphere at the equator and inside it at any other latitude, so every
  station link would be unavailable. The design keeps the link's own rule and
  notes that a positive elevation clears the planet. So a `DataLink` leaves a
  station end to its mask, which the graph checks first; the planet is convex,
  so a line of sight above the local horizon does not cross it. A link kind of
  your own that tests occultation needs the same care; its sample tells a
  station end by its model (`sample.spacecraft isa GroundStation`) and its
  negative index.
- **F2, the planet's frame inside a step (D-I9-4).** In a station run, the
  force, data and received-power passes read `_planet_lpi_at(p, t)` at each
  right-hand-side evaluation, through the run's planet-frame cache when it has
  one (by default, quaternions every 30 s, interpolated), so a station moves
  through a step. Availability and the policies read `planet.L_PI`, which the
  planet-frame callback has just set at the accepted step. The test runs a
  pass's rising half with 30 s steps: the bits the spacecraft sent match a
  quadrature of the closed-form rate with the frame at each time to `1e-7`, and
  the same quadrature with the frame held at each step's start misses by more
  than `1e-4`. The cache's frame and the ephemerides' differ by about `3e-11`.
- **F3, the data of I-9a.** The design gives the data to and from stations to
  I-9b. A station link that can be scheduled carries data through the existing
  data pass, so this PR counts it at the spacecraft end, as I-4 counts any
  link: a downlink in `data_sent_bits`, an uplink in `data_received_bits`. A
  station keeps no counters yet; I-9b adds them, with I-8's stores.
- **F4, the policies at a station end.** A station has no orbit and no battery:
  - `SemimajorAxisRatePolicy` and `EccentricityRatePolicy` never take a station
    end as a target (`:both` takes the spacecraft end, `:lower_index` the
    spacecraft end of a station link);
  - `state_of_charge(ctx, -s)` is NaN, so `EnergyBalancingPolicy` scores a
    station link 0;
  - a caller's `candidate_force(target, partner, link)` receives a station
    partner as its state sample, where a spacecraft partner is its run state.
- **F5, equal antennas.** As for `terminal_number`, two antennas with equal
  fields are the same value, so an endpoint given as an antenna object that
  matches two is refused: give the number.
- **F6, names.** `add_ground_station!` refuses a second station with the same
  name, which the design did not require: I-9c shows a station end by its name
  in the schedule table.
- **F7, checkpoints.** A run with stations records each station's site and mask
  (each float with its bits) and its antennas (as terminal identities), so a
  resume with any of them changed is refused. The name is a label and is not
  recorded. A run without stations writes the configuration it wrote before.
- **F8, the viewer.** The viewer's interlink block refused a schedule naming a
  spacecraft outside the scene, so a station run's block would have failed.
  It leaves station links out until I-9c draws stations; the link schedule
  shows a station end as `-s`.
- **F9, validation.** `lat_deg` within [-90, 90], `lon_deg` and `alt_m` finite,
  `min_elevation_deg` within [0, 90) (a negative mask would no longer be a line
  of sight), at least one antenna, all `RadioTerminal`s. At setup, a station
  antenna's link level must be within its limits, as a spacecraft terminal's
  must, though a station draws nothing.
- **Low, by design:** a single elevation mask per station (D-I9-5); antennas
  steer freely, with no pattern or limit (D-I9-3); no station power (D-I9-6);
  a pass starts and ends at the scheduler's accepted steps.

## Residual Risk

- **Cost.** A station run evaluates the planet's frame at every
  right-hand-side evaluation. With the planet-frame cache turned off
  (`SPACEAGORA_PLANET_FRAME_CACHE=0`) and SPICE ephemerides, that is a SPICE
  frame call per evaluation. Station candidates add an elevation check each per
  accepted step, O(stations × spacecraft) when every pair is registered. Not
  measured on a large constellation.
- **Link kinds of your own** that test occultation must leave a station end to
  its mask (F1).
- **The viewer** shows no station links until I-9c.
